## 1 · The routing principle — sovereign by default

**Every feature routes to the Protocol Plane unless it provably cannot.**

§22 already splits the platform into Fiat (custodial, KYC) and Protocol (non-custodial, permissionless), and `checkAccess` already short-circuits `custodial:false` + `protocol` to `allowed.permissionless` **after** region screening. That machinery exists and is tested. What has been missing is a rule about which plane a _new_ feature lands on.

The rule, from here:

> A feature goes on the Fiat Plane only if it **must hold user value** or **must know who the user is** to function. If it can work against an account the user controls, it goes on the Protocol Plane.

This is not a preference for decentralisation as an aesthetic. Custody is the single largest driver of what we are legally, operationally, and morally on the hook for. **A feature that never holds value cannot lose it, freeze it, or be compelled to seize it.**

### What this means in practice

| capability             | plane    | why                                                      |
| ---------------------- | -------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| copy execution (§2)    | Protocol | user's own account signs; we never hold                  |
| smart-account trading  | Protocol | already there                                            |
| deposits / withdrawals | Fiat     | we hold value at the boundary — unavoidable              |
| fiat ramps, cards      | Fiat     | a counterparty requires identity                         |
| OTC escrow             | Fiat     | escrow **is** custody, by definition                     |
| algo (TWAP/VWAP)       | Protocol | pure execution scheduling over an account we do not hold |

**Deposits and escrow are honestly Fiat and should stay there.** A spec that claims escrow is non-custodial is lying about the word.

## 2 · Copy execution — the architecture

The user's own smart account executes. We never take custody, and we never take discretion beyond the exact envelope the user signed.

### The primitive already exists

`contracts/SessionKeyLib.sol` — `assertGrantable`, scoped session specs, and `SmartAccount.sol` already forbids a session key targeting the account itself. Copy execution is a **session key with a strategy-shaped scope**, not new custody machinery.

### Hard requirements

1. **The follower's account holds the funds.** Always. There is no platform account, no omnibus, no pool. If two followers' funds ever sit in one account, this is a fund and the spec has failed.
2. **The session key is scoped and capped** — permitted markets, max notional per order, max aggregate exposure, expiry. Enforced **on-chain in the account**, not in our service. A cap only we enforce is a promise; a cap the account enforces is a fact.
3. **Unilaterally revocable, without our cooperation.** The follower can revoke directly against their own account even if every service we run is offline. **If revocation requires us to be alive and willing, it is custody wearing a costume.**
4. **The leader never touches follower funds.** The leader signs their _own_ trades. We observe, and each follower's account independently executes a scoped mirror. The leader has no key, no claim, and no ability to withdraw.
5. **No discretion of ours.** We execute the envelope. We do not size, time, skip, or improve a copied trade beyond the follower's stated caps.

### Failure posture

If a copy cannot be executed honestly — cap exceeded, market closed, key expired, chain unreachable — it **refuses and says why**. It never partially fills into a different shape and calls it the same trade, and it never silently drops. A follower who believes they are copying and is not is being deceived, whatever the logs say.

## 3 · Paying the leader — the actual answer

**Share our fee. Never the follower's profit.**

This is the whole design, and it is the difference between an introducing-broker arrangement and an unlicensed investment manager.

### The model

We already charge maker/taker in bps (`ratesForFill`). A leader earns a **share of the protocol trading fee generated by the volume they originate**. The follower's profit and loss is untouched — 100% of gains and 100% of losses are theirs.

```
leader_earnings = Σ (follower_fill_notional × protocol_fee_bps × leader_share_bps)
```

Note what is absent: **no reference to P&L anywhere in that formula.**

### Why this survives when a profit share does not

A performance fee is compensation **for the outcome of someone else's capital**. That is the thing licensing regimes are built around, and non-custody softens it but does not remove it. A high-water-mark structure — the classic "20% of profits above prior peak" — is the _literal_ structure of a hedge fund fee, and adopting it invites exactly the characterisation we are trying to avoid.

A volume-based fee rebate is **compensation for originating order flow**. Introducing brokers, affiliates, and referral programmes have run on this for decades, openly. It is a share of _our_ revenue, not a claim on the user's money — the leader is paid by us, out of fees we already charge, for business they brought.

The follower's position is strictly better too: **their upside is never diluted.** A leader who makes them 40% takes none of it.

### The obvious risk, and the mitigation

Volume-based pay incentivises **churn** — a leader can farm fees by overtrading, and the follower pays the cost in spread and fees while the leader gets paid either way. This is real and must be designed against, not disclaimed:

- **Cap leader earnings per follower per period.** Past the cap, extra volume earns the leader nothing, so churn stops paying.
- **Decay the rate with turnover.** The first N round-trips per period earn full share; beyond that the rate falls. Genuine strategies rarely need extreme turnover; fee farming requires it.
- **Publish realised follower net performance next to leader earnings**, always together. A leader earning well while followers lose should be _visible_, not buried.
- **Fee-share is killable per leader** from the kill-switch surface, like every other route.

### Optional second leg

A **flat subscription** — follower pays the leader a fixed amount per period for access. Also not performance-linked, also safe, and it gives leaders predictable income. Can coexist with fee-share.

**Not permitted in v1:** any fee computed from follower P&L, in any form — percentage of gains, high-water mark, hurdle rate, or "success fee". If we ever want that, it is a licence conversation first, code second.

### The economics — why this is more profitable, not a compromise

The instinct is that giving up a performance fee gives up money. It does not, and the reason is worth stating because it decides how the parameters get set.

**A performance fee is paid by the follower out of gains. A fee-share is paid by us out of revenue we already collect.** The first is a transfer from the customer; the second is a customer-acquisition cost. So the right comparison is not "20% of profits vs a few bps" — it is **fee-share against every other way we would have spent to acquire that follower.**

A leader who brings 200 followers is a distribution channel. Paying them a slice of the fees those followers generate is the cheapest acquisition we have, it is **self-funding** (it only pays out when revenue exists), and it **cannot run at a loss** — unlike paid marketing, which spends whether or not anyone trades.

**Parameters, and the constraint that sets them:**

| parameter          | constraint                                                                                                 |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `leader_share_bps` | a **share of our fee**, never a markup on it — the follower must pay the same fee copying as trading alone |
| earnings cap       | per follower per period — the churn brake                                                                  |
| decay threshold    | round-trips per period before the rate falls                                                               |

**The load-bearing one is the first.** The follower must pay **exactly the same trading fee** whether they copy or trade alone. We fund the leader from our existing take, and we do not raise fees on followers to cover it.

The moment we mark the fee up for copiers, the follower is paying the leader after all — and we have rebuilt the performance fee's economics with a worse story, because now it is opaque. **Same fee, funded from our side, is what makes the "we pay for order flow" characterisation true rather than a label.**

`leader_share_bps` is a revenue decision and sits in §8 with the other fee recipes. It is not agent-settable, and it should be set once with the acquisition-cost comparison above in front of you rather than by analogy to competitors' profit splits.

## 4 · What we deliberately do not build

These are cheap to add and expensive to have added:

- **No leaderboard ranked by returns.** A ranked list of traders sorted by performance is a **recommendation**, and it is ours, not the user's. Followers may search, filter, and view a leader's full verified history — including drawdown and loss periods — but we do not sort the world by who made the most money.
- **No "recommended for you" / "top trader" surfacing.** Same reason, worse.
- **No pooling.** Ever. See §2.1.
- **No platform-run strategies.** If we run it, we are the manager, and none of this analysis applies.
- **No performance projections.** Past results shown must be actual, verified, and include losses.

**These are self-imposed and they are the load-bearing ones.** Non-custody plus a volume fee is a strong posture; adding a returns-ranked leaderboard on top substantially weakens it, for a feature nobody needs.

## 5 · Honest limits — say these in-product

- Copying is **not** advice, management, or a promise. Say it plainly where a user acts, not only in terms.
- A follower's caps are **theirs**, enforced by **their** account. We cannot raise them and cannot override them.
- **We can go offline; their revocation cannot.** That is the point.
- Region screening runs **before** the permissionless short-circuit, and that ordering is deliberate: sovereign does not mean unscreened.

## 6 · Open — owner and counsel, not engineering

Build proceeds on the §2–§4 shape regardless, because it is a strict subset of any answer below.

1. **Which jurisdictions are served, and which are screened out.** Passive availability with honest geo-gating is defensible; gating while marketing into the same market is not. **This is the single highest-leverage answer.**
2. **Does volume-based fee-share survive in the target jurisdictions?** My read is that it is materially safer than a performance fee, and that is a design judgement, not a legal opinion.
3. **Is a searchable directory of verified leader history "advice"?** We have already given up ranking; whether the directory itself crosses the line is a counsel question. If it does, the fallback is a leader address the follower supplies — strictly worse product, still shippable.

**What is not open:** the platform never takes custody, never takes discretion, never charges on P&L, and never ranks. Those are settled here and are not agent-reversible.

## 7 · Build order

1. Session-key scope + on-chain caps in `SmartAccount` (extends the existing lib) — **the whole safety model is here; nothing else ships first**
2. Unilateral revocation path, proven with every service stopped
3. Copy observation → per-follower scoped execution, with typed refusal
4. Fee attribution: notional by originating leader, off the existing fee surface
5. Fee-share payout as a **ledger recipe** (§0.6 — it is revenue share, so it is a recipe like any other)
6. Churn caps and rate decay
7. Directory: verified history, **no ranking**

Class M throughout — it moves value. Nothing here is agent-mergeable under the §3 carve-outs in `DIRECTION-2026-07-31.md`.
