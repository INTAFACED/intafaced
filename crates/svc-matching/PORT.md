# svc-matching in Rust: the port notes

Each slice of svc-matching's move to Rust starts here, in a fresh thread. `services/svc-matching` stays the service that runs until the switch. This crate holds the shell every slice builds on:

- `src/env.rs`: the environment, held to `env.ts` by `tests/env_parity.rs`. There is no `DATABASE_URL`.
- `src/errors.rs`: how each door refuses, as `router.ts` writes each body. The user copy lives in `src/errors_table.rs`.
- `src/door/`: every route `index.ts` serves, who may call each, and each door's input checks before it reaches the engine (`schemas.json`, run with `src/refines.rs`).

`scripts/record-door.mjs` writes the recorded files from the TypeScript. Its `--check` fails when one is stale.

## What the shell holds, and how it is proved

- **`tests/wire_replay.rs`** sends every request of `services/svc-matching/src/wire-replay.golden/` to the door. That is 6,246 answers over 46 doors (all but `/ready` and `/metrics`), two set-ups and nine callers.
  - Each answer the door decides must match byte for byte:
    - a service's signature and caller-list refusals (401, 403);
    - the founder's principal and scope (401, 403), and the founder door's 503 while unconfigured;
    - Fastify's 414 for a long path parameter;
    - every 400 of a door's input checks.
  - Any other answer must be one the door names as its slice's (`Outcome::Owed`).
  - `WIRE_REPLAY_SLICE=<name>` prints what a slice owes. `WIRE_REPLAY_STRICT=<name>` fails while it owes any.
- **`tests/input_parity.rs`**: 724 inputs over the 15 door schemas (`tests/fixtures/input-parity/`, one file a schema), against zod's own verdicts. The lifecycle proof's `superRefine` hashes the parsed snapshot as `snapshotIdOfParsed` does. It also holds 20 list windows against `parsePublicL2QueryLimit`, the 1-to-500 edges included.
- **`tests/env_parity.rs`**: 43 environments, against `env.ts`.
- **The book** (`src/book/`, `src/engine/`, `src/door/book.rs`): the replay owes it nothing (`WIRE_REPLAY_STRICT=book`), and the TypeScript tests are ported test for test:
  - `tests/book.rs`: `book.test.ts`, `book-clone.test.ts`, `depth-memo.test.ts`, `book-l2-depth-unset-refuse.test.ts`.
  - `tests/engine.rs`: `engine.test.ts`, `gtd-journal-clock.test.ts`, the holey tape of `journal-gaps.test.ts`, and the two constructor-refusal files.
  - `tests/order_rules.rs`: `fok`, `ioc`, `min-qty`, `min-notional`, `self-trade`, `maker-or-cancel`, `collar`, `post-only`, `reduce-only`.
  - `tests/engine_calls.rs`: `mass-cancel`, `close-position`, `l3-queue`, `post-only-market`, `reduce-only-market`, `core-tif`, `reconcile.test.ts`.
  - Each file's tests not ported say why at the top: they test a later slice's door or wrapper, a helper no door calls, or what a Rust type already holds (a required depth window, a required snapshot cadence, a TIF that is an enum).
  - Where the Rust differs:
    - The book holds every order type `book.ts` holds, but the door serves only this slice's orders. A submit naming a stop or trigger, an expiry date, an OCO, a bracket or a trail is owed by `triggers`; a strike, expiry, combo or legs by `spreads`.
    - A cold start stops, rather than rebuild a book without the module that reads it, on a journalled order with such a part, a `mark` or a `spread_list`.
    - The market modes are read back from the journal and gate every submit and amend; the doors that switch them are the `venue` slice's. The `open_surveillance` evidence records `surveillance-persist.ts` appends are the `venue` slice's.
    - SBE links no encoder: `/depth/sbe` and `?format=sbe` answer the TypeScript's 409 `matching.sbe_unavailable` without its Java bridge.
    - The mass cancel is cod-fence's, as the TypeScript serves it: it journals `mass_cancel` and then cancels each order. In maintenance every cancel is refused live, but replay of that record cancels them; the port keeps this, and it is the `venue` slice's to settle.
- **The serve slice** (`src/main.rs`, `src/status.rs`, `src/holds.rs`, `src/bus.rs`, `src/engine/admission.rs`): the replay owes it nothing (`WIRE_REPLAY_STRICT=serve`; `/health`'s percentiles are written as 0, as the recording wrote them), and:
  - `tests/serve.rs` ports `health.router.test.ts`, `operation-latency.test.ts`, `metrics.test.ts` (through the server's routing), `replay-hold.test.ts`, `replay-hold.boot-wrapper.test.ts`, `expiry-sweep.test.ts` and `shutdown.test.ts`, with `/ready`. Its top says which tests are not ported and why.
  - `tests/binary.rs` runs the binary on a journal with one resting order, against a stand-in NATS and with no `LEDGER_URL`: it replays, fails the hold check closed, serves `/health`, `/ready` and `/metrics`, and on SIGTERM drains and exits 0 with the journal's lock released.
  - Where the Rust differs:
    - The door times the three operations itself, from the request's arrival at the door; Fastify's `elapsedTime` also counts reading the body off the socket.
    - The drain disables the engine once the signal is seen (the sweep's loop watches for it, every 50 ms), lets the doors in flight finish, then closes the bus and the journal. The TypeScript disables first and then closes the app.
    - A publish counts as timed out when its error names a timeout, or the socket's "temporarily unavailable" or "would block", as a read past the client's bound reports it.
    - Ignoring `SIGXFSZ` is a handler that does nothing: a write past `RLIMIT_FSIZE` comes back short and the journal refuses it.
- **Liquidity** (`src/engine/liquidity.rs`, `src/door/liquidity.rs`, and the in-flight marks in `src/engine/mod.rs`): the replay owes it nothing (`WIRE_REPLAY_STRICT=liquidity`), and the TypeScript tests are ported test for test:
  - `tests/liquidity_book.rs`: `iceberg`, `peg`, `aon`, `auction`.
  - `tests/liquidity_engine.rs`: `ifm`, `ifm-crash`, `mass-quote`, `mmp`, `auction-uncross`, `liquidity`.
  - `tests/liquidity_doors.rs`: the `iceberg`, `peg`, `aon`, `ifm`, `auction` and `mass-quote` router tests.
  - Where the Rust differs:
    - The TypeScript wrappers install by import; here they are the engine's steps in the wrappers' order: a submit is checked for a sidecar or vendor maker, then MMP, then a crash-window mark, then the auction state, before the base engine's gates.
    - A cold start no longer stops on an `in_flight` mark the journal never resolved: it marks that order unknown, and its every submit, amend and cancel refuses `in_flight_unknown`, as `ifm-crash.ts` does.
    - The TypeScript tests' injected throws are made by a journal that refuses a record or a bus that refuses an event. A depth window is a required `usize`, so `sourcedDepth` without one is held by the type.
    - Nothing serves `uncross`, `enterAuction`, `leaveAuction`, `sourcedDepth`, `payRebate`, `qualityTelemetry` or `applyMmp`; they are ported as the engine's calls, each refusing while its owner value is unset. `bulk-items.ts` is no door's and no slice's here.
- **The journal** (`src/journal/`):
  - `tests/journal_codec.rs`: 870 records against `encode`'s own bytes, every kind with each optional field absent and set to each kind of JSON value, 23 of them refused as `encode` refuses; and 23 bodies against `decodeAll` (torn, broken and blank lines). `scripts/record-journal.mjs` writes `tests/fixtures/journal-codec.golden.ndjson`; its `--check` fails when it is stale.
  - `tests/journal_file.rs`: `journal.test.ts` test for test, its children run as this test binary under the same `RLIMIT_FSIZE` launcher.
  - `tests/journal_replay.rs`: `journal-gaps.test.ts`, and replay's rules over a book that writes down what it is given.
  - Where the Rust differs: a record whose `marketId` is not text refuses the replay (the TypeScript opened a book under that key); dropping a `FileJournal` releases its lock as `close` does. A process under `RLIMIT_FSIZE` gets `SIGXFSZ` on a short write unless it ignores it, as Node and the test launcher do: the binary ignores it.

The TypeScript replay runs its doors in a fixed order on one engine. A valid submit rests, a halt halts, and later doors read what earlier ones left. `tests/wire_replay.rs` replays the same way: each set-up on its own engine, the doors in the matrix's order, each input from every caller in turn. `set` starts from its seed's two journal records (the market opened, one sell resting) read back by a cold start. Once a door this port does not serve yet answered a write (a 200 that is not a refusal), a later answer that differs is owed by every slice that wrote before it; an answer that differs with no such write before it fails the test. The engine slices (`journal`, `book`, `triggers`, `liquidity`) also prove themselves with the TypeScript engine tests under `services/svc-matching/src/engine/`, run against the Rust engine.

## The doors

Every service door goes through `requireService` in `router.ts`:

- the caller's key, or the shared secret while the mode allows it;
- `index.ts` passes body binding `require`, so an unbound v1 signature is refused;
- the verified service must be on the door's list: `TRADE_ONLY` (svc-trade) or `BASKET_CHILD_CALLERS` (svc-trade, svc-execution, svc-fix);
- a refusal is 401 `Unauthenticated` with why, or 403 `Forbidden`.

Every founder door (`/operator/*`) goes through `founderPrincipal`. It needs the edge-signed principal (`EDGE_PRINCIPAL_SECRET`, or `EDGE_PRINCIPAL_PUBLIC_KEY` with its limits), with `admin:read`, or with `admin:write` and a second factor. The public reads check no one.

A refusal inside the engine is a 200 with `accepted: false`, not a 4xx. Only a malformed input is a 400.

There are no tables. Each market's book lives in memory. Every input is written to the append-only journal (`MATCHING_JOURNAL_PATH`, newline-delimited JSON, fsynced) before the book moves, and a cold start replays it. Journal record kinds:

`submit`, `cancel`, `amend`, `mass_cancel`, `session_dead`, `mark`, `halt`, `resume`, `halt_all`, `resume_all`, `reduce_only`, `resume_reduce_only`, `post_only`, `resume_post_only`, `prelaunch`, `open`, `expire`, `delist`, `maintenance`, `end_maintenance`, `split_brain`, `clear_split_brain`, `in_flight`, `open_surveillance`, `spread_list`, `rulebook_version`, `book`.

Events go out on the bus from `engine.ts`: `orderAccepted`, `orderFilled`, `orderCancelled`, `orderTriggered`. Spread fills go to `spreadFilled` (`spread-fill-publisher.ts`).

"May call" is each door's guard in `router.ts`. The callers named beside it come from `tooling/shape/call-graph.json` and the paths each caller's code names.

## What the calls share

- **One engine.** Every slice reaches the books through one `MatchingEngine`, made once in the binary and passed in (`docs/agents/shape.md` rule 5). The TypeScript installs its order types and policies by import (`install*` in `index.ts`); the port builds them as parts of the engine instead.
- **The journal comes first.** A cold start replays the journal before the port opens. Replayed resting orders are then checked against the ledger's holds (`LEDGER_URL`, `replay-hold.ts`, `open-after-replay.ts`). With no hold check the book stays closed, `/ready` answers 503 and submit refuses.
- **The kill switch.** `MATCHING_ENGINE_ENABLED` follows the custody switch (`resolveOwnOrderBook`). Disabled, the engine refuses submits before it journals them.
- **Amounts** are decimal strings on the wire and scaled integers inside: the kit's `service_kit::money`, never a float.
- **The lifecycle proof.** Every place, amend, quote and spread order carries svc-trade's proof. The door checks its schema, its snapshot hash, its market and its action; the engine trusts nothing else about the market's state.
- **Identity approvals** (`IDENTITY_URL`) are spent by every venue mode door, every founder brake, a spread listing and a rulebook publish. Without `IDENTITY_URL` the consumer is the unwired one, which refuses.
- **Owner values stay blank.** `MATCHING_SNAPSHOT_EVERY` has no default, every list's window is published per request (1 to 500), and MMP magnitudes and maker identity refuse while unpublished.

## Slices

- `journal`: the append-only journal (`journal-codec.ts`, `journal-io.ts`, `journal-persist.ts`, `journal-gaps.ts`, its file lock and fsync), the snapshot and its sink, and replay's own rules (`journal-replay.ts`) over the books it is handed, held by the TypeScript journal tests. Ported: `src/journal/`
- `book` (after `journal`): `journal-wire.ts` (`fromWire`, `toWire`), the books replay walks (`ReplayBooks` in `src/journal/replay.rs`) and replay to the exact book, held by the TypeScript replay tests that need a book (`journal-gaps.test.ts`'s holey tape, `engine.test.ts`, `oco-replay.test.ts`, `gtd-journal-clock.test.ts`); `POST /markets/:marketId/orders` for limit and market orders with GTC, IOC, FOK and post-only, self-trade prevention, minimum size and notional, price collars; `DELETE /markets/:marketId/orders/:orderId`, `PATCH /markets/:marketId/orders/:orderId`, `GET /markets/:marketId/orders`, `POST /reconcile`, `GET` and `POST /markets/:marketId/positions/...`, `POST /markets/:marketId/orders/mass-cancel`, `GET /markets`, `GET /markets/:marketId/depth`, `/depth/l3`, `/depth/sbe`, and the order events. Ported: `src/book/` (the book, its rules and the journal wire), `src/engine/` (the engine and reconcile), `src/door/book.rs`
- `serve` (after `book`): the binary serving the door, `GET /health`, `GET /ready`, `GET /metrics` and the operation latency, the boot (replay, the ledger hold check, `open-after-replay.ts`), the expiry sweep, the drain on shutdown. Ported: `src/main.rs`, `src/status.rs`, `src/holds.rs`, `src/bus.rs`, `src/engine/admission.rs`
- `triggers` (after `book`): stop, stop-limit and take-profit orders, trailing stops and `POST /markets/:marketId/mark`, OCO and brackets, good-till-date expiry
- `liquidity` (after `book`): iceberg, peg, all-or-none, IFM, the auction and its uncross, liquidity, `POST /markets/:marketId/mass-quote` with MMP. Ported: `src/engine/liquidity.rs`, `src/door/liquidity.rs`
- `venue` (after `book`): `POST /markets/:marketId/halt`, `/resume`, `/reduce-only`, `/reduce-only/resume`, `/post-only`, `/post-only/resume`, `/prelaunch`, `/open`, `/expire`, `/delist`, `POST /halt-all`, `/resume-all` with the identity approval they spend, the halt law, `POST /session/dead` and cancel-on-disconnect, `GET /surveillance/cases`, `POST /surveillance/adjudicate`, `/fine`
- `spreads` (after `book`): `GET /spreads`, `GET /spreads/:spreadId/book`, `POST /spreads/:spreadId/list`, `POST /spreads/:spreadId/orders`, the combo and option books, and `spreadFilled`
- `rulebook` (after `book`): `GET /rulebook`, `GET /rulebook/versions`, `POST /operator/rulebook/check`, `POST /operator/rulebook/publish`, and the rulebook and certificate refusals in the engine
- `operator` (after `venue`): `GET /operator/in-flight`, `POST /operator/halt`, `/resume`, `/halt-all`, `/resume-all` with their idempotency keys
