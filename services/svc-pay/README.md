# svc-pay

The payments core (§6.1). Merchants, the payment lifecycle, settlement into the ledger, and the `RailAdapter` interface every external rail sits behind.

**What it is NOT:** it does not hold balances (the ledger does), it does not choose between rails (smart routing is its own feature), and it does not know the name of a single payment processor. Every rail — the two here and every one that comes later — is an implementation of one interface, and `src/rails/conformance.ts` is what keeps that true.

**On tip today:** gateway mode, rails (`crypto-native`, `card-sandbox`, absent `bank-payout`), public REST + webhooks, PayFac **sub-merchant trees + area fence on money paths** ([below](#payfac--sub-merchant-trees-61)), subscriptions as **crypto invoice-and-watch product-complete** (mandate � due � invoice � capture settle � cancel immediate; card path refuses `pay.mandate_rail_absent`; bounded dunning � `arrears` stall; pre-charge notify sealed as �13 `socket.pay-precharge-notify` with Ready `subscription.productReady`  never invents `notified:true`), destination shape refuse (EVM + IBAN) before hold, G3 settlement release, G4 suspend-safe payout hold, **PSP digital KYB** (`kyb.submit` / `kyb.decide` under `admin:compliance` + append-only KYB/pricing histories; no third-party money lib  D-S-10).
**On tip for pay.fraud (D26-P1-P5):** scoring + review queue + dispute **case** mechanism (`fraud.*` tRPC); chargeback ledger recipes refuse-closed via named �13 `socket.pay-chargeback-ledger-wire` (recipes exist in ledger-client  not posted from svc-pay). List content Class X.
**Still separate / residual:** smart routing (no invent costs/approval rates), commerce plugins, live card charge-against-mandate + real pre-charge delivery (`socket.psp-partners` / `socket.pay-precharge-notify`), IFSC bank dest without partner table, `pay:*` grant path (Nitro DIRECTION �8.4), `kybStatus` money-gate (pay.gateway  sequenced after approver), card acquiring (`socket.psp-partners`), durable disputes table + owner sign-off to close the chargeback ledger socket.

## The lifecycle

```
created ──authorize──▶ authorized ──capture──▶ captured ──settle──▶ settled
   │                        │                      │                   │
   └──────▶ failed ◀────────┘                      └──refund──▶ refunded ◀──refund──┘
```

`payment_events` is the append-only state history. Every transition appends a row; **nothing is ever overwritten**, and the database enforces that with a trigger rather than trusting the application. `payments.status` is a projection of that log, and the captured and refunded totals are summed from it — there is deliberately no `captured_amount` column, because a running total beside an event log is a second source of truth for money.

Where value actually is, at each stage:

| Stage        | Where the money is                                                                                           |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `created`    | Nowhere. Nothing has moved.                                                                                  |
| `authorized` | Nowhere in the book. A promise (card) or a confirmed on-chain transfer at an address we control (crypto).    |
| `captured`   | `pay:clearing:<merchantId>` in the ledger. Ours to hold, the merchant's to receive, not yet theirs to spend. |
| `settled`    | The merchant's own `available` balance, minus the fee.                                                       |
| `refunded`   | Back out through the rail boundary it came in on.                                                            |

## API

Internal tRPC (`src/router.ts`). Money is a **decimal string** in both directions; the input schema rejects a JSON number.

A merchant's reads of its own account (every `pay:read` row below except PayFac sub-merchant reads, plus the `merchant.*` portal, `merchantOps.*`, `merchantControl.*`, `merchantMetrics.*`, `conversion.*` and `payHalts.status` reads) also accept `pay:onboard` (`src/own-merchant-read.ts`). The owner of a business whose KYB is not approved yet holds only `pay:onboard`, and still sees every portal room in its restricted state. Both scopes are rechecked against the live merchant, so a suspended or closed business loses them at once. Each read keeps its own ownership or membership check; the two platform-wide ones are fenced too (`railHealth` needs a business of the caller's own, `payHalts.status` without a merchant id needs `pay:read`). Writes, refunds and payouts keep their scopes and the KYB money gate, with one exception: the business profile writes (`merchant.businessProfile.submit`, `confirm` and `setSecondApproval`) also accept `pay:onboard`, so the owner can fill in the business details before verification and an admin can change them on a verified business; second approval stays the owner's alone (`profileWrite` in `src/merchant-portal/router.ts`; the rules are on `manageFence` in `src/merchant-portal/profile.ts`).

### Merchant statement (`merchant.statement.list`)

The page is one window, not the merchant's whole history. `from` and `to` are required (`pay.statement_range_required`). The span is at most 92 days (`pay.statement_range_too_wide`). One request resolves at most 2000 candidate ledger keys (`pay.statement_too_large`). Those two numbers are technical safety bounds so a read cannot walk every capture, settlement, refund, dispute, and payout the merchant has ever had. They are not a retention policy and not a fee.

Running balances need a ledger balance at `from`. `LedgerClient` does not expose one, so the response says `runningBalances: "within_range"` and `opening: null`. In that mode each line carries `windowNet`, the signed net of that line's account inside the window after the line (a debit is positive). `runningClearing` and `runningProceeds` are null and `runningReason` is `no_opening`. A negative window net is an outflow recorded without an opening balance, and the line stays `runningOrder: "clear"`.

When the client exposes `balanceAsOf`, `runningBalances` is `from_opening`, lines start from that opening, and `windowNet` is null. A reconstructed balance that would go negative is `runningOrder: "order_ambiguous"` and `runningReason: "order_ambiguous"`. Later lines for that asset drop their balances. The page still returns.

Same-timestamp lines from different ledger transactions follow `seq` or `sequence` when every line at that instant has one, otherwise the hash chain (`previousHash`). When neither a sequence nor a distinct chain position orders those transactions, the line is `order_ambiguous` in either mode.

One `merchant.statement.list` runs per merchant at a time. The slot is a row in `pay.merchant_statement_leases`, kept for at most 60 seconds and deleted when the scan finishes. This process also runs at most two scans at once. A second call for that merchant, or a third scan on that process, is `pay.statement_busy`. Those bounds are a concurrency limit. They are not a quota and not a fee. The scan does not hold a pooled connection across the ledger reads.

`payment.refunds.get` loads the merchant id and fences before it parses an amount or reads the ledger. A malformed row the caller does not own is `pay.refund_not_found`.

`settlement.lifecycle` derives status from the timeline. When the status column is ahead of `settled_at` / `sent_at` / `failed_at` (migration 0073 did not backfill them), the read adds one synthetic event `{ at: null, timeKnown: false }` so the derived status is not lower than the column.

### Developer sandbox TEST funds (`sandboxFunds.fund`)

A signed-in person (`trade:write`) presses Fund on one of their developer sandboxes. Where a sandbox's TEST balances are kept is the owner's decision, so until it is made every call refuses `pay.sandbox_funds_unset` (PRECONDITION_FAILED) and nothing is written: no ledger call, no table. The sandbox belongs to svc-identity and pay never reads its tables; the refusal is the same for every sandbox id, so it reveals nothing about whose an id is.

**Withdrawal limits (D-148, W-withdraw-05).** A per-customer cap by KYC tier, per transaction and rolling 24 hours, per asset, lives in `src/limits/`. It is off by default; `PAY_WITHDRAWAL_LIMITS=required` turns it on. The owner-set figures are rows in `pay.withdrawal_limits`, changed through `founderAdmin.limits.change`: setting a limit or raising either figure needs a second founder (bound to the exact change; `founderAdmin.limits.preview` returns the hash), cutting or clearing one needs one founder, and every change appends to `pay.withdrawal_limit_events`. The old `PAY_WITHDRAWAL_LIMITS_BY_TIER` setting is retired and stops the boot if set. A blank figure refuses (`pay.guard_withdrawal_limit_unset`), a hit refuses with the remaining allowance (`pay.guard_withdrawal_limit_exceeded`, field `remaining` on the refusal, `allowance` on the error data). The rolling sum counts every withdrawal state except `failed` and `canceled`, so a state added later counts until someone decides it should not. `withdrawal.limits` (`trade:read`) tells the caller their tier's figures, what the window has used and the most one withdrawal may be now; it refuses with the guard's own codes. The customer's tier is read per request from identity (`GET /internal/account/:userId`, needs `IDENTITY_URL`); without it `required` refuses every withdrawal with `pay.guard_withdrawal_tier_unwired`, and an identity failure refuses with `pay.guard_unavailable`, never a guessed tier. A founder's limit override for one customer and asset (J-ops-console-04 #12) is read per request from identity too (`GET /internal/limit-override/:userId?rail=withdrawal&assetId=`): it replaces the base figure it names until its stored end time, then the base applies again. It may raise or lower a figure (identity requires the other founder above the owner ceiling), never stands in for an unset base, and an unwired read refuses `pay.guard_withdrawal_override_unwired`. `withdrawal.limits` and the founders' `founderAdmin.limits.customer` (one customer's base, override, used and left, for the console) show both. Also on `withdrawal.create` are the treasury exposure cap (platform-wide per asset, enforced only with `PAY_TREASURY_LIMITS=required`) and, for a user who owns a merchant with a legacy settlement of that asset, that merchant's payout policy: daily payout cap, beneficiary cap and approval threshold (enforced only with `PAY_MERCHANT_CONTROLS=required`).

**Screening desk (W-compliance-notices-02).** A withdrawal the screening guard holds for review becomes a case in `pay.screening_cases`, with the party, amount, asset and provider label saved at screening time and every step appended to `pay.screening_case_events` (append-only). `screening.cases.list` and `.get` read cases and `screening.sanctions.status` says whether the list is blank or named, with no list contents; all need `admin:compliance` in the pay module. `screening.cases.decide` (MFA) either upholds the match (final, one person) or releases it. A release needs a second person bound to the case and the reason (`pay.screening_release`), is refused for a refused case, a blank list, a payout, or when `PAY_SCREENING_RELEASE_TTL_SECONDS` is unset, and is spent once, by the next withdrawal of the same party and asset up to the case amount, before it expires; the release records which send key spent it, in the same transaction as the claim, so a retry of that send after a crash gets the same release again (never a second, never past its expiry or above its amount) and a rolled-back claim gives it back. A send whose own case was refused or upheld is never covered by another send's release. A spent release is the person's decision and is not re-screened at spend, so its time-to-live is the window. `screening.cases.rescreen` asks the screener again and a clear answer releases nothing. Not yet built: the deposit-side kind, `sanctions.list.import`, customer refusal copy, desk screens and payout release.

**Custody account model (W-custody-ops-01).** `founderAdmin.custody.*` (founder-only, `src/custody/`) holds who owns what, never a balance: the ledger stays the only book. `entities.create/list` take an id, name, ownership (`entity_owned` or `customer_attributed`, which needs a customer reference, one entity per customer per environment) and an environment (`sandbox`, `testnet`, `mainnet`; all but mainnet carry `testBadge`). `accounts.list/get` is the ownership read with vault and wallet counts. `vaults.create` is idempotent on the caller's `clientRef` (same key and name returns the vault, another name conflicts), `rename` and `setVisibility` take the version the caller loaded, and a vault is hidden or renamed, never deleted (a trigger refuses it). `vaults.list` needs a page size and a `visible`, `hidden` or `all` filter; `vaults.reconciliation` lists every vault including hidden ones and has no filter. `walletTypes.list` is the network registry as it stands (empty until a founder adds a network). `wallets.add` needs a network and asset the registry lists; a wallet has no address until address assignment (W-custody-ops-03) fills it in, and `wallets.lookupByAddress` returns the wallet, vault and entity or `custody.address_not_found`. Every write appends to `pay.custody_events` (append-only). The host for this module is svc-pay, not svc-ops: it already holds the network and wallet-route registries and the address derivation, and svc-ops keeps the tier shell for W-custody-ops-04. Not yet built: the customer or console screens and per-vault balances (W-custody-ops-04).

**Network fees and gas policy (W-custody-ops-10).** `founderAdmin.custody.fees.*` and `founderAdmin.custody.gas.*` (founder-only, `src/custody/fees/`) hold settings, never a balance. `fees.set` records, per registered network, the default fee level (`low`, `medium`, `high`), a fee cap with its unit, and the managed-nonce switch; every write is appended and the latest row is the setting (`get`, `list`, `history`), and a network with none reads `configured: false` with null figures. A cap is a required positive decimal, so a blank one is a parse error and a send on a network with no cap refuses (`custody_fees.cap_unset`). `fees.estimate` returns the three tiers and whether each is inside the cap (null when there is no cap or its unit differs); the estimate comes from a `FeeEstimator` port, and with none wired in this process it refuses with `custody_fees.estimator_unwired` and never invents a figure. `fees.check` is the check a send makes before it signs: a fee above the cap refuses (`custody_fees.fee_above_cap`), as do an estimate in another unit and a down estimator; a fee equal to the cap passes. `gas.policy.get/set` keeps the fee payer (`self`, `station`, `relay`), gas threshold, gas cap, max gas price and the auto top-up switch, all required positive figures; the view names `rail.crypto` as the reason an automatic top-up or a relayed send does not run from here. `gas.refuels.record/list` writes down a refuel that happened elsewhere, idempotent on its reference. A retried send keeps its nonce because the signed raw transaction, nonce included, is journalled before the broadcast (`src/rails/broadcast-store.ts`). Not yet built: a chain estimator and the rail's use of `fees.check` and the nonce switch (both sit behind `rail.crypto`), the gas station tank balance (read from the chain, never kept here), the fee level on withdrawal create (W-withdraw-04), and the finance screens.

**Custody transaction policy (W-custody-ops-07).** `founderAdmin.custody.policy.*` (founder-only, `src/custody/policy/`) keeps ordered rules in versions (`pay.custody_policy_versions`). `draft` saves the one open draft (pass `expectedDraftVersion`, null to open one); `validate` returns per-rule problems. There is no direct publish: a founder proposes `founderAdmin.custody.config.propose` with scope `policy` and payload `{ version, rulesHash }` (both on the version view), the existing W-custody-ops-06 quorum of registered approvers (never the initiator) approves it, and the task's applier (a `policy` task with no `payload.kind`; a named kind such as `sweep_policy` routes to that kind's applier) publishes that draft, refusing if it is no longer the open draft or its rules no longer hash to what was approved. `approvalTaskId` on the version names the task, `publishedBy` the proposer and `approvedBy` the approvers whose decisions carried it. A published version is immutable (database trigger), so a change is a new version and the old one becomes `superseded`. `list`, `current` and `simulate` (decides a transaction against the published or a named version, writes nothing) are reads. A rule matches on assets, networks (for a customer withdrawal: its rail id), destination kinds, destination allow and deny lists, transaction types, initiators, an amount per transaction and a cumulative amount over a window (in the asset's own units; counted by the same D-148 `usedInWindow` definition, windowed on `updated_at`, so a reopened old claim counts as today). Actions are `block`, `approve`, `additional_signers` (both need an approval group with a reachable threshold) and `allow`. Matching rules are read top to bottom: any matching `block` wins, otherwise the first match decides, and no match is a refusal. An empty list, a blank or non-positive amount or window, or an amount in USD or EUR (no price source is wired) makes the rule invalid. Inside the withdrawal claim the guard is on by default (`PAY_CUSTODY_POLICY` unset means `required`; `off` must be set explicitly; any other value stops the boot), so no published policy refuses `pay.guard_custody_policy_unset`, `block` refuses `pay.guard_custody_policy_blocked`, no match refuses `pay.guard_custody_policy_no_match`, and `approve` or `additional_signers` refuse `pay.guard_custody_policy_approval_unwired` until withdrawals are routed into the approval group. Because the guard fails closed, a local stack cannot send a customer withdrawal until a policy has been published through the quorum (or `PAY_CUSTODY_POLICY=off` is set for that stack); a refusal with `pay.guard_custody_policy_unset` there is expected, not a bug. Not yet built: that routing, the check again at signing (W-custody-ops-08) and the `/custody-ops` editor screen.

**Sweeps to treasury (W-custody-ops-05).** `founderAdmin.custody.sweeps.*` (founder-only, `src/custody/sweeps/`) moves coins from customer deposit addresses to the treasury address on chain. It never posts to the ledger and never changes a customer balance (ENGINE D-5). `sweeps.policy.set/get` keeps the owner-set policy per network and asset, append-only, with the latest row in force. Setting it needs a second founder's identity action approval (`pay.custody_sweep_policy_set`, bound to every field; `sweeps.policy.preview` returns the hash), and the approver is stored on the row. The policy holds a trigger (`immediate`, `amount` with a positive decimal, `count` with a count of at least 1, `schedule` with minutes) and the treasury address. A pair with no policy refuses (`custody_sweep.policy_unset`), and a blank or mismatched figure refuses (`custody_sweep.policy_invalid`). `sweeps.due` reports whether the trigger fires over credited deposits that have no sweep yet. `sweeps.create` writes the intent row for one credited deposit under the key `sweep:<depositId>:treasury`, and creating it again returns the same row. `sweeps.execute` claims the row with a conditional update (only `pending`, `failed` or `unknown`), records who ran it, and asks the `SweepBroadcaster` port with that key every time. A definite failure is `failed` and may be retried; a broadcaster that throws leaves the row `unknown`, and the retry uses the original key; `broadcast` is final. With no broadcaster in the process, it refuses `rail.crypto` and the row stays as it was. Not yet built: the chain broadcaster itself (behind `rail.crypto`), a scheduler that acts on `due`, the fee-below-threshold trigger, gas top-up intents, confirmation tracking after broadcast, and the `/custody-ops` screen. The deposit crediting half of this slice is W-deposit-05.

**Withdrawal status, history and eligibility (W-withdraw-06).** `src/withdrawals/status.ts` is the customer status vocabulary (`requested, pending_review, approved, held, dispatching, broadcast, confirming, settled, rejected, canceled, failed, needs_review`) and its transition table. The table still stores four values (`pending`, `held`, `sent`, `failed`); `customerStatus` shows a stored row in the wider vocabulary without rewriting it, so an old `sent` row reads as `settled` and no row loses a hold. A move the table does not list throws `wallet.withdrawal_transition_refused`; the four moves the code writes today are checked before the ledger post, so an illegal one posts nothing. A hold may be released only where no broadcast can have happened (`mayReleaseHold`). `withdrawal.history` (`trade:read`) returns the caller's own withdrawals in that vocabulary (a `held` row split into `held`, `dispatching` or `broadcast` by its current attempt's dispatch journal, the same `shownStatus` as `withdrawal.get`, and a status filter matches that split), filtered by asset, status, id and time, with a keyset cursor (microsecond-exact, so a history over 200 rows pages to the end); `limit` is required and a status no stored value can mean yet returns an empty page. A failure shows plain words from `src/withdrawals/reasons.ts`, or the raw code when it is not one we explain. `withdrawal.eligibility` lists one check per named reason (hold after a password change, open orders, margin, negative balance, frozen credit, removed wallet): only the negative-balance check is answered here (from the ledger); the others read `unwired` and block, because not checked is never fine. Not yet built: the event per transition (needs the events PR first), the migration that stores the wider set (W-withdraw-07, -10 and -11 add the moves that need it), and the status timeline screen.

**Withdrawal fee quotes (W-withdraw-04).** `withdrawal.quote` (`trade:write`, the caller's own id only, `src/withdrawals/quotes/`) previews one withdrawal: the fee parts, whether the fee is on top of the amount or deducted from it, the net amount, the total debit and an expiry, all as decimal strings. The network fee is the registry's own (`fee_estimate_source` `fixed` and its amount); a blank fee refuses `pay.fee_unset`, and a `chain_estimate` or `venue_quote` source refuses `withdrawal_quote.fee_source_unwired` because no estimator or venue is connected here. The fee mode (`on_top` or `deducted`) and the quote lifetime are owner-set under `founderAdmin.withdrawalQuotes.*` (append-only, latest row wins); with none set a quote refuses `withdrawal_quote.policy_unset`. Only the network fee exists today, so bank, conversion and other fee parts are absent from `fees`, never shown as zero. A quote is stored under the caller's `quoteRef` and never changes: a retry reads the stored row, other terms under the same ref conflict, and `WithdrawalQuoteService.accept` marks a quote used by one client reference before it expires. The amount must also fit the network's withdrawal rules (below): a quote below the minimum, above the maximum or off the step refuses `withdrawal_quote.below_minimum`, `above_maximum` or `off_step`, and a pair with no rules refuses `withdrawal_network_rules.unset`, storing nothing. `withdrawal.create` accepts the quote it names inside its claim and posts its fee (below). Not built (named): a send in fee mode `deducted` (refused `withdrawal_quote.fee_mode_unsupported`, never guessed).

**Withdrawal network rules (W-withdraw-02).** `withdrawal.networkRules` (`trade:read`, `src/withdrawals/network-rules/`) reads the owner's rules for one asset on one network: the minimum and maximum one withdrawal may send, the step every amount is a whole multiple of, and the tag rule (`none` or `required`; a send carries no tag, so the Withdraw form refuses a `required` network in words). `withdrawal.create` refuses a destination that names a tag, by name and before anything is claimed or held: `pay.withdrawal_tag_not_taken` on a `none` network or a send with no network (a send inside the platform), `pay.withdrawal_tag_unsupported` on a `required` one (J-wallet-05 #1); the tag is never silently dropped, and a tag or memo under any other key is a bad request. A `required` network gets no quote (`withdrawal_quote.tag_required`), and every send out of the platform must accept a quote inside its claim, so no path sends one without its memo. A pair with no row refuses `withdrawal_network_rules.unset`; nothing has a default (`pay.withdrawal_network_rules`, no row seeded). The owner sets them under `founderAdmin.withdrawalNetworkRules.*` (`list`, `preview`, `save`, `history`), versioned and audited in the append-only `pay.withdrawal_network_rule_events`; publishing a pair, lowering a minimum, raising a maximum, a step that is not a whole multiple of the old one, or dropping a required tag takes a second founder's approval bound to the exact change. The local test stack publishes labelled TEST rules (`local-test-gates.mjs withdraw-terms`).

**Account closure check (RC-wallet-03).** `POST /internal/closure-check` (`src/closure-check.ts`) answers svc-identity's closure question for svc-pay's own tables and nothing else: `open_withdrawal_hold` (a withdrawal still `pending` or `held`), `open_screening_case` (a withdrawal screening case that still needs a person, or a release granted and not yet spent or expired) and `open_deposit_hold` (a deposit the rail took that is still `pending`, not booked). The body is `{ userId }` (identity's `subAccountIds` is accepted and ignored: pay has no sub-accounts); the answer is `{ asOf, blockers: [{ code, count }] }`, the shape svc-bank's check answers, so identity parses both the same way. It is service-signed and only svc-identity may call it (any other signed service is 403, an unsigned caller 401). A failed read is 503, never an empty list, and an empty list means only that pay saw nothing open: ledger balances are svc-bank's `balance_remaining`. It reads and moves nothing. Not built here: the deposit-hold half beyond a pending deposit (W-deposit-06 adds holds), identity calling it (it needs `IDENTITY_CLOSURE_PAY_URL` in svc-identity, RC-wallet-04), and the close-account room.

**UTXO inventory and consolidation (W-custody-ops-13).** `founderAdmin.custody.utxo.*` (founder-only, `src/custody/utxo/`) keeps the unspent outputs of a UTXO-network custody wallet. The outputs come from a `UtxoNode` port; with none wired in this process `list`, `due` and `consolidate` refuse `rail.crypto` and nothing is invented. `list` refreshes from the node and shows txid, vout, amount, confirmations, state (`available`, `reserved`, `spent`) and an operator label; an output the node no longer reports becomes `spent`. `label` edits only the label (appended to `pay.utxo_label_events`). The owner decides when to consolidate per network with `setTriggers`: a minimum count of confirmed available outputs, a low-fee level with its unit, or both; with neither set `due` and `consolidate` refuse `utxo.triggers_unset`, and a network registry row with no confirmation depth refuses `utxo.confirmations_unset`. A fee trigger fires at or below the level only when the estimator answers in the same unit; an unreadable estimate is a note, never a yes. `consolidate` takes a business `intentRef`: under a per-wallet lock it reserves the confirmed available outputs (fewer than two refuses `utxo.nothing_to_consolidate`), writes the intent with the frozen input list first, then asks the node, then records `broadcast` with the txid, or `failed` and frees the outputs on a definite node refusal. Any other node error leaves the intent and the reservation in place (`utxo.outcome_unknown`) and the same reference resumes with the frozen inputs; a node adapter must be idempotent on `intentRef`. Not built: the node adapter and the build and signing of the consolidation transaction (behind `rail.crypto`), a timer that evaluates `due` on its own, and the finance screen.

**Custody transaction history, lifecycle and confirmation policy (W-custody-ops-11).** `founderAdmin.custody.tx.*`, `.reasons.catalog`, `.lifecycle.*` and `.confirmation.*` (founder-only, `src/custody/tx/`). A custody transaction is a row of `pay.withdrawals`; nothing is copied or held. `tx.list` pages every user's withdrawals newest first (the caller says the page size) in the custody states SUBMITTED, PENDING_AML_SCREENING, PENDING_AUTHORIZATION, QUEUED, BROADCASTING, CONFIRMING, COMPLETED, CANCELLED, REJECTED, BLOCKED, FAILED and NEEDS_REVIEW; a travel-rule outcome that blocks a not-yet-final transfer reads as BLOCKED with the rule version and who decided. PENDING_SIGNATURE and SIGNED are in the vocabulary but no stored signal says a withdrawal is in them, so no row is shown there. `tx.get` adds the reason (the catalog text, or the raw code when it is not one we explain), operator notes and block info from a `TxChainReader` port: with none wired the block says `unwired` (`rail.crypto`) and invents no height or hash. `tx.addNote` appends a note; `tx.timeline` merges creation, travel-rule transitions, return events, notes and the current status by time. `reasons.catalog` lists customer and support text per code. `lifecycle.setTimeout` records how long the owner allows in a state (none set means none); `get` and `list` mark a non-final transaction overdue when its last update is older, and nothing moves a transaction on its own. Confirmation policy: the network default is the registry count (blank refuses `confirmation.default_unset`); `confirmation.request` records an override with a reason, `approve` needs someone other than the requester, a count below the default also needs an expiry, and zero is allowed only for one transaction whose destination is one of our own custody wallets. An override applies to custody only; `creditConfirmations` (what a customer credit waits for) reads the registry and nothing else. Not built: the chain reader, the send path reading `confirmationPolicy`, the rule that checks an override's network against the withdrawal's own, a timer that acts on overdue, and the support and finance screens.

**Per-vault queue, stuck transactions and reverse (W-custody-ops-12).** `founderAdmin.custody.queue.*` and `.stuck.*` (founder-only, `src/custody/stuck/`). `queue.link` ties a withdrawal to the custody wallet and nonce it was signed with (the send path will call it; an operator can too); `queue.list` shows a wallet's held, linked transactions lowest nonce first with the head of line marked, and `queue.diagnose` names the stuck head of line, other stuck transactions and any with no nonce. What counts as stuck is the owner's per-network threshold (`stuck.setThreshold`); with none set the list says `stuck: null` and diagnosis refuses `stuck.threshold_unset`. `stuck.act` takes a boost, replace or drop with the caller's own `actionKey`: the action (original hash, nonce, optional fee ceiling and unit) is written BEFORE the `TxReplacer` port is asked, the port must be idempotent on the key, a definite refusal such as `GAS_PRICE_TOO_LOW_FOR_RBF` is recorded as `failed`, and any other error leaves the intent so the same key resumes. The same key twice is one action; with no signer connected it refuses `rail.crypto` and writes nothing. `stuck.resolve` asks the `NonceChain` port and records `replaced`, `dropped` or `original_mined` only when the chain shows it; for a DROP the original counts as dropped only when the nonce was taken by the drop's own transaction, never on a plain eviction, because an evicted transaction can be rebroadcast and mined. `queue.cancel` calls the customer cancel (W-withdraw-07) for an operator, so a held or sent withdrawal is refused there. A reverse (`stuck.requestReverse`, `approveReverse`, `executeReverse`) needs a drop action whose outcome is `dropped`, evidence equal to that action's hash and nonce, a reason and an approver other than the requester; the evidence is about one withdrawal attempt (links, actions and requests carry it; a customer retry opens a new attempt, so old evidence reverses nothing there), and `executeReverse` asks the chain once more and refuses if the original now shows confirmed; it then calls `UserMoneyService.reverseDroppedWithdrawal`, which, given the approved attempt, under the withdrawal's payout lock posts `withdrawReverse` under the attempt's own key (a rail refusal uses the same key, so it posts once), requires the row to be `held` with its hold on the ledger, and stores it as `failed` with `custody.dropped_by_chain`. Not built: the signer and chain reader adapters (behind `rail.crypto`), the send path writing `queue.link`, a timer that raises the stuck alert, and the screens.

**Outbound travel rule (W-compliance-notices-04).** `src/travel-rule/` keeps one record per withdrawal whose status is its latest transition (STARTED, PENDING, REVIEW, REPAIR, ACCEPTED, COMPLETED, REJECTED, plus `not_required`, `provider_unavailable`, `blocking_time_expired`, `canceled`, `failed`). The owner decides every figure and list under `founderAdmin.travelRule.*`: per (region, entity, asset) whether the rule applies, the amount threshold and the IVMS101-shaped fields to collect, and the pending and repair wait limits. With no rule `wallet.travel_rule_undecided`; with no wait limit a required transfer cannot be opened (`travel_rule.wait_limit_unset`); a rule that does not apply is the only way a transfer is `not_required` (shown as Bypassed, stored with the rule version), and no operator can move a transfer there, to accepted or to completed (`travel_rule.operator_cannot_bypass`). `message.create` stores an AES-256-GCM encrypted message (key in `PAY_TRAVEL_RULE_MESSAGE_KEY` base64 and `PAY_TRAVEL_RULE_MESSAGE_KEY_ID`; unset means `travel_rule.cipher_unwired` and nothing is stored; set wrongly fails boot), takes the amount, asset, network and address from the stored records, and sends the transfer to REPAIR when a required field is missing. A send with no counterparty network records `provider_unavailable` and refuses `vendor.travelrule`. A wait past its limit becomes `blocking_time_expired` (`expireDue`), which still blocks; a timer never approves. The customer reads their own `travelRule.status.get`. `TravelRuleService.assertMayBroadcast` is the gate the rail send must call before signing: only accepted, completed or not required passes. Not built (named): calling that gate and opening the record from the withdraw door, with the customer questionnaire (W-withdraw-16, which also needs the customer's region and entity from identity); the counterparty adapter (`vendor.travelrule`); compliance-plane reads (founder-only today). The inbound side is the next paragraph.

**Inbound travel rule on deposits (W-deposit-07, W-compliance-notices-05; J-wallet-03 #1-#3).** `src/custody/deposits/travel-rule.ts` and the deposit book. Nothing here posts. The owner sets, per asset, whether the rule applies to an inbound deposit, its threshold and the fields to collect (`founderAdmin.depositTravelRule.rules.set`, one founder with a reason, appended; the latest row per asset is the rule). A deposit that is otherwise due (confirmed, at or above the minimum) is judged against it: with no rule for its asset it is not credited and stays `confirming` with reason `travel_rule_unset` (judged again on each pass), so production deposits wait until the owner sets a rule for each asset; below the threshold, or under a rule that does not apply, it credits as before; at or above the threshold it is `held_travel_rule` (reason `travel_rule_declaration`) and a record opens in the same transaction. A held deposit follows its confirmations and is never credited by a pass. The depositor reads and answers it (`custodyDeposit.travelRule.get/declare`): the sending wallet type and every asked field, sealed with the travel-rule key bound to the deposit id (only the asked fields are kept, nothing is sent back; no key refuses `travel_rule.cipher_unwired` and stores nothing). Compliance may wait on the counterparty (`markCounterpartyPending`, the owner's pending wait limit; blank refuses `travel_rule.wait_limit_unset`); a wait past its deadline is recorded `blocking_time_expired` by the next pass and the deposit stays held. A founder releases a declared deposit (`release`; undeclared refuses `travel_rule.not_declared`): the release and the deposit's return to judging are one transaction, and it is credited once under its usual `deposit:crypto-native:...` key (a ledger timeout keeps the credit started and the pass finishes it). A held deposit the chain takes back is closed `reversed` and its record refuses further moves. Not built (named): a reject path that returns the coins, the counterparty network (`vendor.travelrule`), and a second founder on release.

**Purchase holds (W-withdraw-13).** Funds bought by card, ACH or PayPal are tradable but cannot be withdrawn until released. `founderAdmin.purchaseHolds.*` (founder-only, `src/withdrawals/purchase-holds/`) records a hold (`record`, idempotent on the caller's hold reference such as `card:<payment id>`; the same reference with other terms conflicts), lists them, and sets and reads the owner's release period (`setPolicy` in whole seconds, appended and never edited; `policy` reads the latest). With no period set there is none: nothing is released and a withdrawal of held funds refuses with `pay.purchase_hold_period_unset`, never a guessed time. `releaseDue` writes one release row per hold whose period has elapsed, so it releases once however often or concurrently it runs; the withdrawal door and the eligibility read run it first. Nothing is booked and no balance lives here: the ledger stays the balance and a hold is a limit on what may leave. A fresh `withdraw` is refused with `pay.withdrawal_amount_held` (the held and withdrawable figures in the detail) when it asks for more than the ledger's available balance minus what is held; the check runs in the claim transaction under a per-customer-and-asset lock and counts the customer's other claimed (`pending`) withdrawals, so parallel asks under different references cannot together take held funds, and a refusal leaves no row and no ledger move. A retry of a withdrawal that already has a row is never refused by a later hold. `withdrawal.eligibility` gains a `purchase_hold` reason that blocks only when nothing of the asset is free to withdraw. Not yet built: the hold bucket in the ledger (a named ledger-owner job, X-6) and the call from svc-bank's card on-ramp that records the hold (needs the contracts or events PR first, so today only a founder records one), and the customer and support screens.

**Multi-approval for withdrawals (W-withdraw-11).** A withdrawal that the owner's policy sends to approvers is submitted as a request (`withdrawalApproval.propose`, `trade:withdraw`) that freezes its amount, asset, rail, destination and client reference; `approve` and `reject` (`trade:withdraw`, step-up), `cancel` (`trade:write`), `get`, `list` and `listForTeam` (`trade:read`) are in `src/withdrawals/approvals/`. The policy is per customer and asset and set by a founder through `founderAdmin.withdrawalApprovals.setPolicy` (approvals needed, the team account, the threshold, the window, the expiry and whether the amount is held at submission; every field is required and appended, never edited; `active: false` clears it). With no policy a request refuses `withdrawal_approval.policy_unset` and withdrawals stay direct. Approval is needed when the withdrawal plus the same customer's other withdrawals and waiting requests of the asset inside the window reach the threshold, so a split cannot slip under it; the direct door (`withdrawal.create`) then refuses `withdrawal_approval.required`, and also refuses any client reference that names a request which has not reached its approvals. Whether a person may approve is svc-bank's (team roles): `BANK_URL` points at it and the call is `business.approverEligibility` from `@intafaced/contracts`; with no `BANK_URL`, an unreachable bank or an unreadable answer every approval refuses (`eligibility_unavailable` or `eligibility_unwired`) and nothing is counted. The initiator can never approve or reject, one approver counts once however often they approve, and any eligible rejection ends the request. With `holdAtSubmit` the amount is held once at submission (the withdrawal is claimed and held and stays `pending`, so the existing cancel gives the hold back once); without it nothing is held and no rail is called until the last approval. After the last approval the frozen terms run through `withdraw` under the same client reference and so the same ledger keys; a crash before the dispatch leaves the request `approved` and `founderAdmin.withdrawalApprovals.settleDue` (or another approval call) resumes it. `settleDue` also expires waiting requests and finishes any release a crash left. It runs on a timer only when the owner sets `PAY_WITHDRAWAL_APPROVAL_SWEEP_MS` (never defaulted); until then the founder call is the only trigger. Not yet built: the customer's `pending_review` status in the history list (read `withdrawalApproval.get` for the waiting state), account-admin self-service for the policy, and API-key withdrawals (W-withdraw-12), which must go through this gate.

**Business top-up (M-money-out-02).** `merchantControl.topUp.options` (the merchant's own read, `src/merchant-pay-dev-out/top-up.ts`) is what a top-up of the business balance can use. svc-pay has no rail that brings money in for a business, so after the ownership check (another business and an unknown id get the same `pay.merchant_not_found`) it always refuses `pay.topup_unconfigured` (412). It posts nothing, reads no balance and invents no address, reference or amount; the portal's `/pay/money/top-up` shows the form with Request off. Not yet built: the top-up rail, its answer shape, the request itself and its ledger-client credit recipe (keyed on the rail's own reference).

**Home work list (HM-dashboard-03).** `workItems.list` answers Home's `payout_waiting` kind (contract `@intafaced/contracts/home-work-items`) for the signed-in person: one item per settlement of their own `active` business that has posted and is not paid out, or whose payout failed and may be sent again (not one released before it posted), oldest first, linked to `/pay/payouts/<settlementId>`, with the net as a decimal string and a failed payout's code as `reasonCode`. It is an `action` for a session holding `pay:payout`, `info` otherwise. A settlement holding a sandbox payment is left out (a live payout of it refuses). Derived on each read from `pay.settlements` (`src/work-items.ts`); nothing is stored. Any signed-in person may ask, through the same gate as the other own-merchant reads (`pay:onboard` / `pay:read`, the owner grant recheck, and the jurisdiction matrix for `pay`): a session with no business, one that gate refuses (no usable pay scope, a withdrawn owner grant, a KYC tier or region kept out of `pay`), or a sandbox key gets `{ items: [] }`; a failed grant recheck or read is an error, never an empty list.

**Internal settlement (W-withdraw-14).** A withdrawal to an address that is another account's deposit address on the platform (`pay.custody_deposit_addresses`, this service's own table: an exact match as stored, except that an EVM `0x` address matches in any case; indexed on `lower(address)`) settles inside the book: `withdrawal.create` takes this branch for such an address whatever rail is named, and `railId: 'internal'` asks for it outright (an outside address then refuses `pay.internal_destination_external`). One of the caller's own addresses refuses `pay.withdrawal_self_send`; a frozen address, one past its expiry on every row, or one more than one account holds, refuses `pay.internal_destination_unavailable` (with `reason` `expired` or plain `unavailable`, so a freeze is never disclosed); all before anything is claimed, and a frozen address still finishes a send already asked of svc-bank. The asset must be one svc-bank takes (`pay.internal_asset_unsupported`). The chain door (`preflightWithdrawal`, which an approval request also runs) refuses a platform address with `pay.withdrawal_platform_address`, so nothing goes out on a chain to come straight back. The claim is the ordinary one with rail `internal`, so approvals, the allowlist, screening, the limits, the merchant controls and the halts all run; the wallet door (stored chain destination, network fee, travel rule, cooling, cap on holds), the custody transaction policy and the treasury cap do not, because nothing is signed and nothing leaves custody (an open internal withdrawal is not exposure). A frozen account is refused by svc-bank, which asks identity whether the sender can send. No hold is placed and no rail is asked: svc-bank's `transfers.settleWithdrawal` (contracts, `BANK_URL`) makes one person-to-person send keyed on the withdrawal row (`withdraw:<id>`). The send is journalled `requested` before svc-bank is asked, so the row cannot be cancelled from then on and a retry is a recovery; `posted` turns the journal and the row `accepted` and `sent` together (`rail_ref` is the ledger transaction), `refused` turns them `refused` and `failed` with svc-bank's code (`pay.internal_settlement_refused`, nothing to put back), and no answer keeps the row `pending` (`pay.rail_outcome_unknown`) until a retry with the same client reference finishes it under the same key. Without `BANK_URL` it refuses `pay.internal_settlement_unwired` before a claim. `withdrawal.destination` tells the customer where an address leads before they send; it never names the other account and never says an address is frozen.

**Cancel and returned withdrawals (W-withdraw-07).** `withdrawal.cancel` (`trade:write`, the caller's own id only) cancels a withdrawal that has no send attempt on record: a `pending` row, or a `held` row whose dispatch journal proves nothing was sent (it moved into `held` with `dispatch_journaled` set, which `withdraw` does because it journals every send as `requested` before it asks the rail, and its current attempt has no journal row). It takes the same payout lock as `withdraw`, reads the ledger for the attempt's hold (a read that fails refuses the cancel), writes the cancel code on the still-`pending` row before it posts anything (so a crash part-way can never let a retry of the same client reference send the funds the reverse returned), posts `withdrawReverse` once if the hold was booked before a crash, and then stores the row as `failed` with the code `withdrawal.canceled`, which history shows as `canceled`. A `held` row with a `requested` journal row (a send whose reply was lost) stays held until the original outcome is known; one with an `accepted` row was broadcast; a `held` row from before the marker (dispatch history unknown, ENGINE S-10), a `sent` row and an already failed row also answer `pay.withdrawal_not_cancellable`. `withdrawal.get` and `withdrawal.mine` add `customerStatus` (a held row reads `held`, `dispatching` or `broadcast` by its journal) and `cancellable`, and the cancel's answer says `holdReleased`; a second cancel returns the same record; the same client reference never reopens it (`pay.withdrawal_canceled`). `founderAdmin.withdrawalReturns.record/get/list/recredit` (founder-only, `src/withdrawals/returns/`) records a sent withdrawal whose funds came back, one per withdrawal, idempotent on the return reference, with every write appended to `pay.withdrawal_return_events`. `record` needs a `reasonCode` (`destination_rejected`, `network_returned`, `other`). A recorded return is parked until a second founder approves it: `recredit` by the founder who recorded it refuses `withdrawal_return.same_founder`, and by another founder writes the approval (`pay.withdrawal_return_credits`, append-only) before it posts `recipes.withdrawReturned` once with the withdrawal's rail and `<id>:<attempt>`, so svc-ledger books it only against that attempt's posted settle (`ledger.withdraw_return_unbound` otherwise). The posted transaction goes in `pay.withdrawal_return_postings`. A lost ledger answer refuses `withdrawal_return.ledger_outcome_unknown` and leaves the return `crediting`; a retry by either founder posts the same key and finishes it. The view's state is `parked`, `crediting` or `credited`. `withdrawal.history` adds `returnedFunds` (amount, reason code, credited) to the person's own row, which the withdrawal page shows as "came back to us, we are checking it" and then "came back to your available balance". Not built: the founder console screen for record and recredit.

**Why these live here.** Value entering the book must come from a rail and value leaving goes out through one; the `RailAdapter` interface, the registry and both v1 adapters are in this service. The alternative was a second service learning about rails, or a money path with no rail behind it — which is a money path reconciliation has nothing to check against.

**Why the withdrawal is not in svc-trade.** `services/svc-trade/src/router.ts` says `trade:withdraw` "appears nowhere here, deliberately: it is an INTERACTIVE_ONLY scope that no API key may hold, which is what protects a leaked bot key from moving value off the platform." That reasoning is about the **surface**, not about where the scope lives — svc-trade is the exchange API that bots hit with long-lived keys. It is respected rather than worked around: the withdrawal lives where the rails live, and svc-trade stays a pure exchange API.

#### `deposit.credit` — operator-credentialed, never user-facing

A user who can call the thing that credits their own balance does not need to deposit. So:

- **`admin:treasury`**, which is in `INTERACTIVE_ONLY_SCOPES`. Both halves of that protection apply for free: a long-lived API key may **never** hold it, and a session without 2FA may not exercise it. `pay:write` would have let any merchant credit any user; `admin:write` is broad and not interactive-only.
- **`creditedBy` comes from the token**, never the body. Every unit of value entering this way names the operator who asserted it arrived.
- **Only rails on `PAY_OPERATOR_CREDIT_RAILS`** (blank refuses; owner may set `card-sandbox` explicitly). A hand-typed `crypto-native` credit would move `railBoundary('crypto-native')` away from the chain balance it mirrors, and reconciliation would report a discrepancy that is really a typo. Misconfiguring it fails at **boot**, not at request time.
- **No jurisdiction guard, and no tier check on the payee.** The matrix judges the user being served, and the principal here is an operator who is not the beneficiary — checking their tier measures the wrong person. And money that has already reached a rail must always be bookable: refusing to credit an unverified user does not undo their payment, it strands it at the boundary. The gate belongs on what a balance can **do**, not on being allowed to receive one.

#### `withdrawal.create` — the interactive path off the platform

- **`trade:withdraw`**, INTERACTIVE_ONLY: no API key may hold it, and `requireScope` refuses a session that has not passed 2FA. A normal session does not carry it at all — `auth.stepUp` in svc-identity mints the five-minute token that does.
- **`{ module: 'ledger' }`, not `{ module: 'pay' }`.** The matrix rule governing a user moving their own custodial balance is the rule for the module that **holds** it, and the ledger holds it (`custodial: true`, `OPEN_BASIC`). `pay`'s `full` tier governs merchant acquiring — third-party card money — a different risk and a different subject. Gating a withdrawal above the tier that admitted the value would build a one-way door: verified enough to deposit and trade, not verified enough to leave.
- **`clientRef` is required, not optional.** A timed-out request retried without one opens a second withdrawal, and a second withdrawal is a second debit. `clientOrderId` is merely recommended on an order because a duplicate order can be cancelled; there is no cancelling a payout.
- **The account comes from the token.** There is no `userId` input, so there is nothing to forge.
- **A send out of the platform names its network and its accepted quote (J-wallet-05 #6, issue #6517).** `networkId` and `quoteRef` are required for a send to a wallet outside the platform; a send inside it (W-withdraw-14) takes neither and has no network fee. Before anything is claimed (`src/withdrawals/send-out/door.ts`):
  1. **A custody signer for the network.** No live signer exists anywhere. `PAY_TEST_WITHDRAW_SIGNER_NETWORKS` (comma-separated network ids; blank or unset is none) connects a TEST signer per network that sends through the existing `crypto-native` rail; svc-pay refuses to boot with it unless `APP_ENV` is exactly `dev` or `test`, and `signerFor` asks again at every send (`src/withdrawals/send-out/signer-posture.ts`, the two-gate pattern of `rails/posture.ts`). No network named, another rail, or a network with no signer refuses `pay.wallet_withdraw_unavailable`, the code the customer app words as "no custody signer is set up". The local stack connects TEST Ethereum only. That TEST signer sends through the in-memory chain, which two TEST settings pace so a real send can be watched on its page (J-wallet-05 #8, `rails/posture.ts` `memoryChainPaceFromEnv`): `PAY_TEST_CHAIN_SEND_DELAY_MS` (whole milliseconds, at most 12000, under svc-edge's 15-second upstream timeout) makes each send wait after it is recorded before it answers, so the page reads Sending meanwhile; `PAY_TEST_CHAIN_REFUSE_AMOUNT` (an exact decimal) refuses every send of exactly that amount before anything is recorded, which `crypto-native` returns as `rail.refused`, so the hold is reversed in the same call. Either set outside `APP_ENV` exactly `dev` or `test`, beside a live chain, or with a value that does not parse refuses to boot. The local stack sets 6000 and 13.
  2. **A quote named** (`withdrawal_quote.required`).
  3. **The address saved, approved and past its cooling-off on that network.** svc-bank owns the address book; svc-pay asks `ramps.destinations.sendable` (packages/contracts `bank-send-destination.ts`) and never reads its tables. Refusals: `pay.withdrawal_destination_not_saved`, `_network_mismatch` (saved only on another network), `_unapproved`, `_cooling`, `_cooling_unset`, `_locked` (the security lock), `_blocked`, `_unavailable` (no answer).
  4. **The quote, in the claim.** In the claim's own transaction: this person's quote, for the same asset, network and amount (`withdrawal_quote.terms_differ`), not used by another withdrawal (`already_used`), not expired (`expired`), fee mode `on_top` (`fee_mode_unsupported`); it is accepted under `clientRef` with the row, which stores `network_id`, `quote_ref` and `fee` (migration `20261029053203`). A refusal anywhere in the claim accepts nothing.

  **Confirm later (W-withdraw-06).** With the advance worker on, a create of a send out of the platform books the hold and answers `held`, cancellable while nothing is journalled; it asks nothing of the chain. The worker (`src/withdrawals/advance-worker.ts`) takes each held send on once it has stood one tick, sends it under the same payout lock and business keys as the create (`advanceWithdrawal`), and asks the chain how deep it is (`RailAdapter.payoutDepth`, `CryptoChainPort.sendDepth`). Below the owner's `PAY_MIN_CONFIRMATIONS` the row stays `held` and reads `broadcast` at depth zero and `confirming` above it (`pay.withdrawal_rail_attempts.confirmations`); at the minimum it settles once. A refusal on the worker's first send reverses the hold, fee included, because the row's journalling proves nothing was sent before; a depth the chain cannot say leaves the hold and is never reversed. `PAY_WITHDRAW_ADVANCE_INTERVAL_MS` and `PAY_WITHDRAW_ADVANCE_BATCH_LIMIT` are owner-set; either blank turns the worker off with a named blocker (`pay.withdraw_advance_interval_unset`, `pay.withdraw_advance_batch_limit_unset`), and a create then sends in the request, still settling only at the minimum depth. `PAY_TEST_CHAIN_BLOCK_MS` (1 to 60000, `APP_ENV` `dev` or `test` only) gives the in-memory chain one TEST block per that many milliseconds after a send answers. The local stack sets 3000, 3000 and 20.

  The fee rides `withdrawHold` / `withdrawSettle` / `withdrawReverse` from the stored row on every attempt, retry, crash recovery, cancel and dropped-send reverse, never from a quote read again: the hold is amount plus fee (the quote's total debit), the settle sends the amount to the rail and the fee to `houseFees('pay')`, a reverse returns both. The rail is asked for the amount alone, and `pay.rail_amount_mismatch` compares its receipt with the amount alone. svc-ledger refusing a fee that is not its hold's (`ledger.withdraw_fee_unbound`) is the definite `pay.withdrawal_fee_unbound` with an alert in the log, never `pay.ledger_outcome_unknown`. The 24-hour withdrawal limit counts amount plus fee; the per-withdrawal limit is the amount sent.

  The old `WalletPay` door (`failClosedWalletPublish`) is gone from the send path: it read a feature status, a per-account switch, a stored destination with a cooling window, a cap, a shown fee, travel-rule fields and an allowlist callback that no source ever published, so it refused every send. What covers each now: the signer posture (the shut door); svc-identity's account freeze at sign-in and at svc-edge (the per-account switch); svc-bank's address book via `sendable` (stored destination, cooling); the D-148 limits guard (cap); the accepted quote (shown fee); the W-withdraw-10 allowlist and W-withdraw-11 approvals guards (allowlist). The outbound travel rule (`assertMayBroadcast`, W-withdraw-16) is not wired to the send: deferred by the owner (owner decision 2026-10-27: no compliance work before launch), and no live signer exists, so nothing leaves custody in staging or prod.

### Which rails are real — read this before believing a `sent`

| rail            | `mode`                      | counterparty                                                                                  | what a `payout` returns                                       |
| --------------- | --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `card-sandbox`  | `sandbox`, permanently      | a `Map` in `card-sandbox.ts`                                                                  | `po_<settlementId>` — a string this file invented             |
| `crypto-native` | derived from the chain port | `EvmLiveChain` when configured; else `MemoryChain` (dev) / `UnconfiguredChain` (staging/prod) | a real `txHash` on a live chain; `0xout…` / refusal otherwise |
| `bank-payout`   | `absent`, permanently       | nothing — sponsor bank / licence is a commercial socket                                       | never succeeds; `pay.rail_not_live` before any hold           |

**Live crypto-native** is wired when all of these are set:

```bash
PAY_CRYPTO_RPC_URL=…          # archive-capable JSON-RPC
PAY_CRYPTO_CHAIN_ID=…
PAY_CRYPTO_DEPOSIT_SOURCE=testnet # deposit addresses, test chains only; a provider source is injected at boot
PAY_CRYPTO_SIGNER=testnet     # test chains only; a provider signer is injected at boot
PAY_CRYPTO_ASSETS=ETH:native  # or ETH:native,USDT:0x…:6
```

That builds `EvmLiveChain` (`src/rails/evm-chain.ts`, `posture: 'live'`), starts the in-process watcher that POSTs signed webhooks with `PAY_CRYPTO_WEBHOOK_SECRET`, and makes `crypto-native` a live rail. Staging/prod then **omit** `card-sandbox` by default (`shouldRegisterCardSandbox`) so the boot posture gate can pass without `PAY_ALLOW_SANDBOX_RAILS`.

**Why sandbox gates still matter.** A withdrawal settled against a sandbox rail debits the user's real ledger balance, writes a fabricated reference into `withdrawals.rail_ref`, and answers `status: 'sent'`. The books still balance — only custody reconciliation can see the lie.

**The two gates** (`src/rails/posture.ts`):

1. **Boot.** `APP_ENV` of `staging` or `prod` with any sandbox rail registered **refuses to start**, unless `PAY_ALLOW_SANDBOX_RAILS=true` says so by name (logged loudly on every boot).
2. **Runtime.** `payout` and `refund` re-check at the call site, **before** the ledger moves — `withdrawal.create` refuses with `pay.rail_not_live` before a row or a hold exists.

`dev` and `test` without live config keep `MemoryChain` + `card-sandbox` as the fixture.

**What the owner must still obtain for production money:**

- A **production** (or public testnet) RPC, not only compose anvil — anvil proves the wiring; it is not a chain decision (`docs/decisions/local-dev-chain.md`).
- A real provider for the hot wallet signer and the deposit address source (HSM or signing service). Neither a key nor a seed phrase is a setting; the testnet implementations refuse mainnet, staging and prod.
- Live boot wires `PostgresBroadcastStore` (`pay.crypto_broadcasts`, migration 0004 + `signed_raw` in 0012) for multi-replica claim→putSigned→sendRaw→put. `MemoryBroadcastStore` remains the unit/local default when no store is passed to `defaultChainFor` in `dev`/`test`. staging/prod live chain **refuses to build** without a store — two replicas on the in-memory journal can double-send. DIRECTION §3.1 / D26-P1-P9: the signed raw is journalled **before** `eth_sendRawTransaction`; crash-resume rebroadcasts the identical payload (`claim` → `resume`) instead of signing a second spend. Put-before-receipt still closes the wait-for-inclusion window.
- Live boot also wires `PostgresChainWatcherCursorStore` (`pay.chain_watcher_cursors`, migration 0017) so the in-process watcher persists last-seen (block, tx hash, log index). A crash that re-drains the same finalization does not POST a second credit. No live chain config → no live chain → watcher never starts; `/pay` deposits stay a named refuse, not an invented inbound.
- **Card acquiring** — sponsor bank / BIN. Still a §13 commercial socket; `card-sandbox` is never it.

`/ready` and `railHealth` both carry `mode` now, because `healthy: true` was accurate and useless — a sandbox is reliably healthy at simulating.

> **Interface mismatch, flagged not papered over.** `RailAdapter.payout` takes a `SettlementInstruction`, which is merchant-shaped — it is the only payout shape §6.1 has. Every adapter uses `settlementId` purely as the payout idempotency key and reads `merchantId` not at all, so a user withdrawal passes its own id and the user id and works correctly. Generalising it to a `PayoutInstruction` is a change to a reviewed interface plus its conformance kit, so it belongs in its own PR (§15.2).

### PayFac — sub-merchant trees (§6.1)

A merchant may now have children. `merchants.parent_merchant_id` is the tree (NULL = top of its own tree, which is what every merchant already was), and `merchants.settling_party` is the spec's one hard design constraint made a column rather than an assumption — `docs/SPEC-PAY-VERTICALS-2026-08-02.md` (removed) §2: _"model the sub-merchant relationship so the settling party is a field, not an assumption."_

**This slice moves no value.** No ledger client, no recipe, no balance, no amount. A sub-merchant is a sovereign account exactly as a merchant is; the tree decides **who may ask**, never where money sits (Doctrine §0.6).

| Procedure                       | Scope       | Input                                                    | Output              |
| ------------------------------- | ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| `submerchant.create`            | `pay:write` | `{ parentMerchantId, userId, pricing: { feeBps }, … }`   | `SubMerchant`       |
| `submerchant.list`              | `pay:read`  | `{ merchantId, limit? }`                                 | `SubMerchant[]`     |
| `submerchant.get`               | `pay:read`  | `{ merchantId }`                                         | `SubMerchant`       |
| `submerchantPermission.grant`   | `pay:write` | `{ granteeMerchantId, subjectMerchantId, area, reason }` | `PermissionEvent`   |
| `submerchantPermission.revoke`  | `pay:write` | `{ granteeMerchantId, subjectMerchantId, area, reason }` | `PermissionEvent`   |
| `submerchantPermission.list`    | `pay:read`  | `{ subjectMerchantId }`                                  | `PermissionGrant[]` |
| `submerchantPermission.history` | `pay:read`  | `{ subjectMerchantId, limit? }`                          | `PermissionEvent[]` |
| `submerchantPermission.areas`   | `pay:read`  | –                                                        | the area vocabulary |

#### The two checks, which are not the same check

1. **Scope** — structural and absolute. The caller's merchant node must be an ancestor of the subject, or the subject itself. **No grant widens this.** A parent cannot read a sibling subtree, a child cannot read upward, and two payfacs are invisible to each other.
2. **Area** — a permission, and only for a subject that is not the caller itself. A merchant holds every area over its **own** node. Over a descendant it holds an area only if it is the **root** of that tree (the node with the platform relationship, which §2 of the spec makes liable for everyone beneath it), or a live grant says so.

**The acting node comes from the token.** There is no `actorMerchantId` on the wire. `pay:write` is a merchant's own scope held by every merchant on the platform, so a merchant node taken from a request body would leave the fence working perfectly while measuring the wrong actor.

**Delegation flows down, and only what you hold.** A non-root node cannot self-grant, cannot grant laterally (one child never gets authority over another), and cannot grant an area it was not given. Onboarding writes exactly two default areas to each intermediate ancestor — `merchant.profile` and `submerchant`, visibility only. **No value-shaped area is held by any non-root node until somebody grants it by name and says why.**

**`pay.merchant_permission_events` is append-only**, enforced by a trigger. A revoke is a new row. "Who could refund this sub-merchant's payments on the 3rd" is argued from in a dispute, and an editable answer is not evidence.

#### The "14 permission areas" — said out loud

The tracker row is titled _"PayFac mode — sub-merchant trees, 14 permission areas"_. **That list has never existed.** The phrase is one title string copied between `tooling/tracker/features.mjs`, `tooling/coverage.yaml`, `INTAFACED_DEFINITIVE_BUILD.md` and three board renders derived from them; `docs/PAY-LANE-HARVEST-AND-BUILD-PLAN-2026-08-08.md` (removed) §2 found the same thing independently and §6.3 puts _"enumerate them, or drop the claim from the title"_ on the owner's list.

So this ships the **mechanism**, and `PERMISSION_AREAS` in `src/submerchants.ts` is **eleven** areas — every one that names a surface this service actually has: `merchant.profile`, `checkout.profile`, `payment.link`, `payment`, `payment.refund`, `settlement`, `settlement.payout`, `webhook`, `kyb`, `submerchant`, `permission`. Padding the list to hit a number in a title would be inventing product law inside an implementation. `area` is stored as text, so the twelfth is a one-line change and no migration.

#### What is deliberately NOT here

- **Settling a sub-merchant out of our account.** `settlingParty` accepts `'self'` only and refuses everything else by name (`pay.submerchant_settling_party_unsupported`). That is acquiring — a sponsor bank and an acquiring BIN, which `docs/SPEC-PAY-VERTICALS-2026-08-02.md` (removed) §8 puts on the owner's list and `socket.psp-partners` tracks. The column exists so adopting a partner later is configuration rather than a rewrite; it is not a switch this service can throw.
- **Areas on the gateway surface (W5).** Money and merchant procedures in `router.ts` / `public-rest.ts` call `assertMerchantAreaAccess` with the matching area (`payment`, `payment.refund`, `settlement`, `settlement.payout`, `payment.link`, `webhook`, `kyb`, …). Self still holds every area. A parent without a grant is `pay.submerchant_permission_denied`; a stranger stays `pay.merchant_forbidden`. Pins: `merchant-ownership.test.ts`.
- **Split payments and sub-merchant fee routing.** Ledger recipes, Class M, and the owner's sign-off (`docs/PAY-LANE-HARVEST-AND-BUILD-PLAN-2026-08-08.md` (removed) §6.4).
- **Sub-merchant KYB workflow and document capture.** Top-level merchant digital KYB (submit + operator decide + history) ships under `kyb.*` / mig 0013; document-capture vendor integration and sub-merchant KYB remain residual. Card acquiring stays `socket.psp-partners`.

### Error codes

`pay.submerchant_out_of_scope` and `pay.submerchant_permission_denied` map to **FORBIDDEN**, and they are different questions on purpose: the first says the node is not yours to look at, the second says it is yours to look at and not yours to act on. `pay.submerchant_user_already_merchant` maps to **CONFLICT** — one merchant per sovereign account is a database rule and nothing the caller resends fixes it. `pay.submerchant_cycle` is **CONFLICT** rather than a 500 so the message naming the corrupted node survives to an operator.

Also: `pay.submerchant_grant_lateral`, `pay.submerchant_grant_self`, `pay.submerchant_grant_redundant`, `pay.submerchant_revoke_redundant`, `pay.submerchant_area_unknown`, `pay.submerchant_too_deep`, `pay.submerchant_reason_required`, `pay.submerchant_pricing_invalid`, `pay.submerchant_settling_party_unsupported`, `pay.submerchant_not_onboarded`.

`pay.rail_declined`, `pay.rail_failed`, `pay.capture_exceeds_authorized`, `pay.partial_capture_unsupported`, `pay.refund_exceeds_captured`, `pay.refund_in_flight`, `pay.settlement_in_flight`, `pay.settlement_desynced`, `pay.rail_amount_mismatch`, `pay.invalid_transition`, `pay.nothing_to_settle`, `pay.fee_exceeds_gross`, `pay.merchant_pricing_invalid`, `pay.webhook_invalid`, `pay.webhook_unmatched`, `pay.rail_unknown`, `pay.rail_not_creditable`, `pay.deposit_conflict`, `pay.withdrawal_not_found`, `pay.withdrawal_conflict`, `pay.rail_not_live`, `pay.destination_kind_mismatch`.

`pay.destination_kind_mismatch` is refused **before** any ledger hold: a crypto payout cannot name a bank IBAN (and the reverse). Retrying with the same wrong kind never moves money.

`pay.settlement_in_flight` and `pay.settlement_desynced` map to **CONFLICT**. The first refuses a pre-settlement refund while a pending window has frozen the payment (clearing must not be drained under a frozen gross). The second refuses to post a settlement whose live captured−refunded totals no longer match the freeze — better a stuck pending row than a ledger entry funded by someone else's capture.

`pay.deposit_conflict` and `pay.withdrawal_conflict` map to **CONFLICT**, never BAD_REQUEST: the caller reused a business key for different numbers, and nothing they resend fixes it.

`pay.rail_not_live` maps to **SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE**, never INTERNAL_SERVER_ERROR. The request was well-formed; the platform has no rail that would actually move the money. A 500 reads as "try again", and this is the one condition retrying can never fix.

This is the state history §6.1 requires, and it is the truth this service reasons from. Ordered by `seq` (a `bigserial`), not by `ts`: every event appended inside one transaction shares a transaction timestamp, and a history that cannot order a capture against a refund is not a history.

## Ledger

Every value movement is a recipe. This service holds no balance of any kind (Doctrine §0.6).

| Recipe                 | Reason code               | Accounts                                                               |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `paymentCapture`       | `payment.captured`        | rail boundary → `pay:clearing:<merchantId>`                            |
| `merchantSettlement`   | `pay.settled`             | `pay:clearing:<merchantId>` → merchant available + `houseFees('pay')`  |
| `paymentRefund`        | `payment.refunded`        | clearing (pre-settlement) or merchant available (post) → rail boundary |
| `paymentRefundReverse` | `payment.refund.reversed` | rail boundary → back where the refund came from                        |
| `withdrawHold`         | `withdraw.held`           | available → purpose-keyed hold (payout / withdrawal in flight)         |
| `withdrawSettle`       | `withdraw.settled`        | hold → rail boundary (completed)                                       |
| `withdrawReverse`      | `withdraw.reversed`       | hold → available (refused)                                             |
| `deposit`              | `deposit.credited`        | rail boundary → user available                                         |
| `depositReverse`       | `deposit.reversed`        | user available → rail boundary (rewritten deposit block)               |

The three `withdraw*` recipes serve **both** outbound paths — a merchant payout keyed on `<settlementId>:<attempt>`, and a user withdrawal keyed on `<withdrawalId>:<attempt>`. Same shape, same reasoning, one set of recipes.

For D26-P1-P4, `settlement-ledger.ts` makes that constraint executable: both
`bank` and `crypto` settlement destinations produce only the existing
`withdrawHold` → `withdrawSettle` / `withdrawReverse` requests. Rail adapters
own the destination difference; svc-pay does not assemble entries or invent a
bank-specific book. `bank-payout` remains `absent` and refuses before the hold
until the Class X sponsor-bank socket exists, while configured `crypto-native`
can complete the same recipe plan against a real chain.

**`merchantClearing(merchantId, assetId)`** is a new account constructor: a `module`-owned account per merchant, per asset. It answers "a payment was captured but not settled — whose funds are those?" as a balance rather than an investigation. `sum(merchantClearing(m))` is exactly what svc-pay owes merchant `m` right now, readable from the ledger without touching a single svc-pay table — which is what makes reconciliation between the two meaningful.

### Idempotency keys — all business keys, never random

| Key                                                       | Governs                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `payment.capture:<paymentId>`                             | A payment is captured once, however many times the webhook is delivered.                                                                                                                                                                        |
| `payment.refund:<paymentId>:<refundId>`                   | One refund, one posting. Namespaced by payment so merchant ids do not collide across payments. `<refundId>` is required on tRPC (a retry must carry the same id); only `PayService` itself still derives `<paymentId>:<n>` when none is passed. |
| `payment.refund.reverse:<paymentId>:<refundId>`           | The reversal of that refund.                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `settlement:<merchantId>:<window>:<assetId>`              | A window settles once, per asset.                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| `withdraw.hold\|settle\|reverse:<settlementId>:<attempt>` | One merchant payout attempt.                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `withdraw.hold\|settle\|reverse:<withdrawalId>:<attempt>` | One user withdrawal attempt.                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `deposit:<rail>:<railRef>`                                | A rail reference is credited once, however often it is redelivered.                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `deposit.reverse:<depositId>`                             | A rewritten block reverses that deposit once. A second report returns the first row.                                                                                                                                                            |

> **Why the asset is in the settlement key.** §6.1's settlement is keyed on merchant and window. A merchant taking USDT and BTC on the same day has two settlements, and without the asset in the key the second would find the first's transaction, return it, and strand a whole currency's takings in clearing. The `settlements` table carries an `asset_id` column for the same reason: `gross`/`fees`/`net` are meaningless without one.

### Order of operations, and whose funds are stranded

The direction of the money decides the order:

- **Inbound (capture):** the rail moves first, the ledger books second. We only book value we know has arrived. A crash in between leaves money captured at the rail and not yet in the book — the classic. Nothing is lost: both halves are keyed on the payment id, so re-running finishes the job.
- **Outbound (refund, payout):** the ledger moves first, the rail second. The merchant must be shown to have the money before any of it goes somewhere irreversible; a post-settlement refund they cannot cover fails at the ledger, before the rail is asked. A crash in between leaves the book correct and only the status projection behind. If the rail then refuses, the ledger posting is reversed in the same call.

A refund whose id is already in flight is **refused**, not re-sent. Live crypto receives a durable `refundId` (M226-02) so a process restart reuses the same outbound broadcast key; card-sandbox ignores it. The **service** still refuses re-entry while `refund.posted` has no terminal `refunded` / `refund.reversed` — "send it again and hope" is how one refund becomes two on a rail that cannot dedupe.

### User money: whose funds are stranded, per branch

**Deposit** (inbound — the rail already moved, in the real world):

| Crash point                  | Whose funds, and where                                            | Recovery                                                                      |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Before the claim row commits | Nobody's in the book. Value sits at the rail with no record here. | Re-run. Nothing was recorded, so nothing is inconsistent.                     |
| Claimed, not yet booked      | **The user's** — at the rail, not in the book. Row is `pending`.  | Re-run the same `(rail, railRef)`; `deposits_status_idx` lists exactly these. |
| Booked, row not flipped      | Nobody's. The user has their money; the row is one status behind. | Re-run: the ledger key is identical, so the post is a no-op.                  |

**Withdrawal** (outbound — the ledger moves first):

| Crash point                  | Whose funds, and where                                                                                                | Recovery                                                                                                                                                        |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Before the hold posts        | Nobody's. The balance is untouched.                                                                                   | Re-run on the same `clientRef`.                                                                                                                                 |
| Insufficient funds           | Nobody's. Nothing moved, and it failed **before** a rail was asked.                                                   | The row records `ledger.insufficient_funds`.                                                                                                                    |
| **Held, rail never asked**   | **The user's, immobilised** — out of `available`, in `withdraw:<id>:<attempt>`. The only branch where value is stuck. | Re-run on the same `clientRef`: the hold key is identical so it moves nothing, and the rail is then asked. `withdrawals_status_idx` lists everything in `held`. |
| Rail refused                 | Nobody's. Reversed to `available` **in the same call**, `attempts` advances.                                          | None needed. Retrying takes a fresh hold key.                                                                                                                   |
| Rail sent, settle not posted | Nobody's is lost, but **the platform is short**: the rail paid out and the book still shows a hold.                   | Re-run: the settle posts once, and the rail is not re-asked because its idempotency key is the same string.                                                     |

The one genuinely stuck state is `held`, which is exactly why it is a real status with its own index rather than something inferred.

## Rails

`src/rails/rail-adapter.ts` is the interface, exactly as §6.1 specifies — `id`, `capabilities`, `authorize`, `capture`, `refund`, `payout`, `verifyWebhook` — plus `health()`, taken from the shape `packages/venue-adapter` already uses for liquidity venues.

| Adapter         | What it is                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `crypto-native` | **Real.** Accepts on-chain assets and settles them to the ledger. Talks to `CryptoChainPort`, never to a node directly.                                                                                                 |
| `card-sandbox`  | A **mock acquirer**, not a mock adapter. The state machine, decline codes, refund arithmetic, signed webhooks and idempotency are all real; only the counterparty is simulated. It runs the full flow end to end in CI. |

### `crypto-native`: authorize and capture are not what they are on a card

A chain has no auth hold and nothing to complete. So:

- **authorize** means "a transfer to this payment's acceptance address has reached `minConfirmations`". Below the threshold the answer is `pending`, not `authorized` — a shallow transaction can still be reorganised away, and a merchant who ships against one has shipped against nothing.
- **capture** is an accounting act, and it re-reads the chain rather than trusting a decision made minutes ago. A reorg between the two is caught.

It also handles what payers actually do. A **payment in parts** adds up: every final transfer in the payment's asset counts, an **underpayment** stays pending with what arrived, what is missing and who sent it (the hosted page shows "60 of 100 received, 40 missing"), and the payment authorizes and captures once when the parts reach the amount. **Wrong token to the right address** fails the payment while reporting the address, the transaction and the sender, so the funds are recoverable rather than merely lost; a later transfer in another token is listed, never added. An **overpayment** is booked at what arrived, because booking less would strand the difference. A refund of a payment paid from several addresses refuses (`refund.destination_ambiguous`) rather than sending one payer's money to another.

### Webhook verification

Constant-time, via `crypto.timingSafeEqual`, over the **raw body** signed together with a timestamp. Length is checked separately first, because `timingSafeEqual` throws on a length mismatch and an unhandled exception on a public endpoint is a denial-of-service surface. Non-hex signatures are rejected outright: `Buffer.from('zz','hex')` is empty, and two empty buffers compare equal — a signature of nothing verifying nothing. Stale timestamps are replays. Nothing throws, whatever arrives.

A verified delivery is still shape-checked: an amount that is a JSON number is rejected even when the signature is perfect.

### Adding a rail — the conformance kit (§6.3)

```ts
import { runRailAdapterConformance } from '@intafaced/svc-pay/rails';

runRailAdapterConformance('acme-acquirer', async () => ({
  adapter: new AcmeAcquirerAdapter({ ... }),
  reset: async () => { ... },
  primeAuthorization: async (intent) => { ... },
  signWebhook: (event) => ({ ... }),
  signRaw: (body) => ({ ... }),
  failNext: () => { ... },
  payoutDestination: () => ({ kind: 'bank', ref: '...' }),

```

**Any future adapter must pass this kit before merge.** It asserts identity and capability declaration, health and staleness, the `RailResult` contract (`ok === (status !== 'failed')`, money is always a bigint, a failure always carries a machine-readable code), authorize/capture/refund/payout idempotency on business keys, refund arithmetic including over-refund across partials, webhook verification against tampering, forged and wrong-length signatures, replay, garbage and correctly-signed-but-malformed payloads, and that rail failures surface as results rather than exceptions and leave nothing half-done.

What the kit asserts is exactly what the core is entitled to assume. That is what makes §6.1's "drop in later as adapters with zero core changes" a testable claim instead of a hope.

## USD → USDT conversion (sandbox)

Live mode refuses to boot. With no testnet key, quote and execute refuse. They do not invent a fill.

**Orphan holds.** `founderAdmin.conversion.holds.sweep` (`sweepOrphanHolds`) stays founder-triggered. The same pass also runs every 5 minutes. One replica holds a session try-lock for the pass; a busy lock releases nothing. Run it after any resolve, and after a crash, so a hold whose conversion row is failed or gone is released. The amount released is the hold the ledger still has. A live row, including `placing`, is left alone.

**Late fills.** Detection is not on the quote or execute path, and it does not book. A timer calls `sweepLateFills` once a minute, and a founder can run the same pass with `founderAdmin.conversion.lateFill.sweep` (that call skips the backoff wait). One replica holds a session try-lock for the tick. A busy lock makes no venue calls. Each tick calls the venue for at most 8 rows, so the lock cannot sit in front of quote and execute lookups. The venue search is only `placing_at` minus 1 minute through `placing_at` plus 10 minutes (the window in which the venue could have accepted the order). The same window is what resolve and reconcile use. Each successful check stamps `last_checked_at` and sets the next check at most 1 minute later, then 5, then 15, then 60. The wait is jittered under that cap and is the same on every replica. A venue or rate error does not stamp the row, is logged, and is retried on the next tick. It does not block quotes or executes. Only a found late fill does.

The sweep stops calling the venue for a resolve-absent row 7 days after the absent-resolve confirmation (or `placing_at`, then `created_at`, when that confirmation is missing). It sets `late_fill_check_final_at`. That horizon is technical: it bounds how long a miss is re-queried. It is not a quote TTL. A founder force does not reopen a final row. A fill that appears after the horizon is not auto-detected.

A found fill writes a `pay.conversion_late_fill` audit row and blocks new quotes and executes with `pay.conversion_late_fill_halt`. Nothing is booked. Clearing a conversion halt, including a halt that was set for another reason, does not lift that block.

**Acknowledgement.** Two founders. `founderAdmin.conversion.lateFill.propose` names how the fill was settled (a sentence, not an amount) and returns a hash bound to the audit row. A different founder calls `founderAdmin.conversion.lateFill.confirm` with that hash. Rejecting leaves the block in place. Confirming records the sentence and lets quotes and executes proceed. It does not book the fill and it does not clear a halt.

**Booking.** Separate from a hand-written sentence. `founderAdmin.conversion.lateFill.book.propose` fetches the venue and returns a hash bound to the audit row and that evidence. The body has no amount. A different founder calls `founderAdmin.conversion.lateFill.book.confirm` with the hash. Confirm fetches the venue again and posts only when the evidence still matches. The post is `fxConversionLateFill`. When merchant clearing covers the filled USD, that USD leaves clearing, the merchant is credited the net USDT, and the fee and spread are the original order's, pro-rated by the venue fill. The idempotency key is the audit row id. When clearing cannot cover, or the fill is under the original minimum, the venue legs go to treasury `pay:fx-inventory` only: inventory is short the filled USD and long the gross USDT the venue delivered. The merchant is not credited and no fee is taken. The merchant keeps the USD the absent-resolve already restored. A `pay.conversion_late_fill_shortfall` audit is written. Confirming the book also writes the confirmed acknowledgement, sentence `booked via late-fill book <requestId>`, so the quote halt lifts in that same two-founder step. `PAY_CONVERSION_MODE` still defaults to off. Nothing on the timer posts a fill.

## Kill-switch

`module.pay` in the admin console — the module kill-switch beats every other flag (`packages/config/src/flags.ts`). With it off, no procedure in this service serves. `pay.payfac` and `pay.laneA` gate features not built here.

## Halts

A halt stops one operation (intake, conversion, payout, or settlement) for everyone, one merchant, one user's withdrawal, or one rail. Auto-trip stays off until `PAY_HALT_AUTOTRIP_FAILURES` or `PAY_HALT_AUTOTRIP_RAIL_FAILURES` is a positive integer. There is no default count. With both unset, a payout, withdrawal, or refund does not take the auto-trip lock.

Adapter throws count per occurrence: N retries of one stuck payout can trip the merchant. That is fail-closed and intended. A generic `rail.failed` without a rail-health code never counts.

## Founder merchant controls and admitted payout recovery

`accountControls.getState`, `history` and `change` authenticate the original signed founder principal with existing interactive login scopes. Every request checks current identity entitlement, active interactive MFA session and expiry through identity. Founder entitlement authorizes these controls without granting unrelated broad admin or treasury scopes. Service contexts, non-founders, API keys, subaccount credentials and supplied actor fields cannot provide founder authority. Either founder can act independently; merchant controls do not consume the unrelated KYB or treasury two-person approvals. Legacy `merchantState.set` is the same strict reason/request-UUID/version command as `accountControls.change`; its former shared-operator approval shape is rejected. Legacy status history records `identity.operator_entitlement` as the actual authorizing authority rather than falsely claiming an admin scope.

Changes own the merchant `FOR UPDATE` guard shared by existing new-payment, authorization, discretionary-capture and payout-admission `FOR SHARE` checks. State, attributed append-only history and actor-bound canonical request replay commit together. Refusal/no-op/version-conflict are explicit outcomes. Closed merchants cannot be restored; a restricted pending merchant restores to pending, with its existing KYB unchanged. Authorization refusals never disclose target state. History requires an explicit bounded limit.

A payout freezes its settlement, attempt/business key, user, decimal-string net amount, asset, original rail and destination in an immutable owner record **before** holding funds. Recovery loads that record instead of inferring admission from a hold balance or rereading a mutable destination. Changed recovery inputs refuse before altering the destination store. An old hold without an owner admission is `pay.payout_admission_missing`, requiring an attributed recovery decision rather than a guessed destination. A posted settlement under suspension cannot create a new payout admission.

Each settlement's processing is serialized on a reserved PostgreSQL connection. Rail acceptance/refusal is committed independently before ledger completion and its SQL projection. Original admitted recovery can finish after suspension and after a hold, rail, ledger or projection crash; accepted rails are not re-asked once their durable outcome exists. Refused attempts reverse through ledger-client and advance the attempt once. Every amount remains a decimal string at persistence boundaries and scaled bigint in recipes; these records are instructions and outcomes, never a second balance book.

### Identity decision boundary

Production boot always installs the reviewed identity decision client. Missing identity URL or the distinct pay-owner admission key refuses fresh operations with `pay.identity_admission_unavailable`; there is no local-only boot fallback. Isolated test constructors may omit the port to exercise local merchant guards. Each fresh payment creation, authorization, capture and payout has its own immutable original prepared intent and `identity_requested` marker committed before requesting a grant. Grant/finalization holds the merchant SHARE guard. A merchant restriction takes the exclusive guard and resolves/cancels every pending marked payment and payout intent before committing; missing, incomplete or failed drains refuse the restriction and preserve those records. Cancellation is retained, so a late grant retry cannot acquire fresh authority after the restriction. A lost acknowledgement may resolve to an already-issued original grant and recover that original operation. A grant never describes completed money movement.

Whole-identity serialization also requires the matching durable identity decision service and its freeze guard, configured with `IDENTITY_PAY_ADMISSION_SECRET` matching pay's `IDENTITY_ADMISSION_SECRET`. These owner keys must be distinct from generic read/service and trade keys. Direct tRPC and merchant REST mutations derive credential authority from the real principal; hosted checkout, provider callbacks and scheduled operations use server-derived merchant policy. Hosted checkout commits the original create intent with its prepared payment/session, obtains the create grant before handing out any instruction, and separately admits authorization. A cancelled original create cannot be authorized. Creation accepts an optional request UUID for original recovery; REST maps the existing actor/idempotency key deterministically to that UUID. Direct PayFac create, authorize, capture and payout preserve the original parent actor and session/API-key/subaccount credential through explicit `payfac_credential` authority. Pay owns the live root relation or exact grant-event ID/sequence; it derives and revalidates that proof under the child merchant SHARE guard. Identity checks both the child owner and original parent identity/credential. Root authority uses the real root relation without an invented grant event. An explicit permission revoke takes the same child UPDATE guard and resolves/cancels all matching marked payment/payout intents before appending its immutable revoke event. Missing, failed or incomplete drains refuse the cutoff. The former delegate cannot initiate work through the route after revocation; owned recovery can finish an original resolved grant with its unchanged payload, rail and destination. This does not authorize new operations after cutoff. There is no service reparenting path; any future ancestry writer must adopt this guard and drain protocol.

### Database and recovery operation

1. Apply `0018_pay_founder_controls_admission.sql` through the service migration runner before boot. The runner serializes competing boots and commits DDL with its journal atomically; up/down/reapply is exercised on isolated PostgreSQL. Its down pair removes these records/columns; preserve an encrypted verified backup before reversing populated storage.
2. Configure identity's real URL, matching edge-principal secret and general service credential for live founder entitlement checks. Identity decision traffic uses its separate per-owner admission key.
3. Investigate unresolved prepared/requested records through restricted service operations. Never delete them or release a hold merely because a request timed out; resolve the original identity decision and use the recorded original payout.
4. Set the operator-history retention/export policy and prove backup restore before live launch. Append-only triggers reject edits/deletes; corrections are new attributed actions.

The PostgreSQL suite injects a SQL failure after successful rail and ledger completion, verifies recovery under suspension without a second transfer, verifies hold-crash recovery, changed-destination refusal, concurrent payout dedupe and reversal-projection recovery. Founder/control tests cover version races, both actors, legacy bypass refusal, immutable audit and pending-decision drain failure/cancellation/lost acknowledgement.

## Service keys: ledger calls

The ledger client signs with svc-pay's own key when `S2S_AUTH_MODE`, `S2S_SIGNING_KID` and `S2S_SIGNING_KEY` are set (`dual` also sends the shared secret, `keypair` the key only); unset, it signs with the shared secret as before. Other outgoing calls still use the shared secret and move in their own PRs. Setup and rotation: `services/svc-ledger/CALLER-BINDING.md`.

## Service keys: inbound doors

The internal doors (due-subscription run, subscription cycle journal, capture reconcile, merchant team email claim, owner pay scopes, pay step-up grant, merchant watch metrics) name the caller from its own service key when `S2S_AUTH_MODE`, `S2S_KEYRING` and the signing pair are set: `dual` accepts the key and the shared secret, `keypair` the key only. Unset, the shared secret works exactly as before. A key-signed call that fails is refused and never retried against the shared secret. Setup and rotation: `services/svc-ledger/CALLER-BINDING.md`.
